# French Food

French cooking is a set of habits more than a set of recipes. Season in layers. Rest your meat. Don't crowd the pan. Deglaze everything, because the brown stuff stuck to the bottom is the flavour you paid for.

A note on butter: use the best you can afford, and buy unsalted so you control the seasoning. In recipes like pommes Anna or a beurre noisette, the butter is basically the whole dish.

Two entries in this chapter are products rather than cooked dishes. Saint-Félicien is a cheese and the baguette is bread. I've written both as things you actually do in a kitchen rather than pretending otherwise.

## 1. Saint-Félicien (Baked, with Walnuts and Honey)

Saint-Félicien is a soft cow's milk cheese from the Rhône, richer and runnier than its cousin Saint-Marcellin. You don't make it at home. What you do is heat it gently until it turns into a sauce, which takes about twenty minutes and feels like cheating.

**Serves 4 | Prep 5 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 Saint-Félicien, about 180 g, in its terracotta or wooden container
- 60 g walnut halves
- 2 tbsp honey, chestnut or acacia
- 2 sprigs thyme
- 1 small garlic clove, sliced into slivers
- Black pepper
- Pain de campagne for dipping
- A few slices of dry-cured ham

**Method**

1. Take the cheese out of the fridge an hour ahead. Cold cheese heats unevenly.
2. Heat the oven to 180°C. Toast the walnuts on a tray for 6 minutes, then roughly chop.
3. Score a shallow cross in the top rind of the cheese and slip the garlic slivers into the cuts.
4. Sit the cheese in a small oven dish, tuck the thyme around it, and bake 15 to 18 minutes until the centre is molten and the top is puffed.
5. While it bakes, toast thick slices of bread.
6. Take the cheese out, spoon the honey over the top, and scatter the walnuts.
7. Crack over black pepper and serve straight away with the bread and ham. It sets as it cools, so eat fast.

**Cook's note:** If your cheese came in cardboard, move it to a small ceramic dish first. Cardboard scorches.

## 2. Châteaubriand

A thick cut from the head of the tenderloin, roasted whole and carved for two. The classic sauce is béarnaise, and I've given you a proper one.

**Serves 2 | Prep 20 min | Cook 30 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g centre-cut beef tenderloin, tied into an even cylinder
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 40 g butter
- 2 garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 sprigs thyme
- Salt and coarse black pepper

*Béarnaise*
- 3 tbsp white wine vinegar
- 3 tbsp dry white wine
- 2 shallots, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp tarragon stalks, plus 2 tbsp chopped leaves
- 3 egg yolks
- 200 g clarified butter, warm
- Lemon juice, salt, cayenne

**Method**

1. Salt the beef generously and leave it at room temperature for 45 minutes.
2. Reduce the vinegar, wine, shallots, and tarragon stalks in a small pan until about 2 tbsp of liquid remain. Strain and cool slightly.
3. Whisk the yolks with the reduction over a pan of barely simmering water until thick and ribbony, about 4 minutes. The bowl should be warm, never hot.
4. Off the heat, dribble in the clarified butter while whisking constantly. Season with lemon, salt, cayenne, and the chopped tarragon. Keep it somewhere warm but not hot.
5. Sear the beef in oil in a heavy pan over high heat, turning every 45 seconds until browned all over, about 6 minutes.
6. Add the butter, garlic, and thyme, and baste for 3 minutes, tilting the pan and spooning the foaming butter over the top.
7. Transfer to a 200°C oven for 8 to 10 minutes, until the centre reads 52°C for medium rare. Rest 10 minutes, then carve thick slices across the grain.

**Cook's note:** If the béarnaise splits, whisk a fresh yolk with a teaspoon of water in a clean bowl and slowly beat the broken sauce into it. Works nearly every time.

## 3. Poulet Rôti

Roast chicken. Every French cook has an opinion about it. Mine: dry brine the day before, start breast side down, and don't stuff it with anything that will slow the cooking.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min plus overnight | Cook 1¼ hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1 free-range chicken, 1.6 kg
- 2 tbsp fine sea salt
- 60 g soft butter
- 1 lemon, halved
- 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
- 4 sprigs thyme, 2 bay leaves
- 500 g small potatoes, halved
- 2 shallots, quartered
- 150 ml white wine
- Black pepper

**Method**

1. The day before, pat the chicken completely dry, rub the salt all over including under the breast skin, and leave uncovered on a rack in the fridge. The skin dries out, which is what makes it crisp.
2. Take it out an hour before cooking. Heat the oven to 220°C.
3. Push the butter under the breast skin and smooth it flat with your fingers. Put the lemon, half the garlic, thyme, and bay inside the cavity. Truss the legs.
4. Sit the chicken breast side down on the potatoes and shallots in a roasting tin. Roast 25 minutes.
5. Turn it breast side up, drop the oven to 190°C, and roast another 40 minutes, basting twice with the fat in the tin.
6. It's done when the thigh joint reads 74°C and the juices run clear. Move it to a board and rest 20 minutes, breast side down for the first 10.
7. Skim the fat from the tin, deglaze over a burner with the wine, scrape up the sticky bits, and simmer 3 minutes into a thin jus. Carve, pour, serve.

**Cook's note:** Resting for 20 minutes sounds excessive and isn't. A chicken carved straight from the oven loses its juice onto the board.

## 4. Pommes Anna

Sliced potatoes, butter, salt. That's the entire ingredient list. It bakes into a golden cake with distinct layers, and the only difficult part is patience with the arranging.

**Serves 6 | Prep 25 min | Cook 55 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg waxy potatoes, peeled
- 180 g clarified butter, melted
- Fine sea salt
- White pepper
- 2 sprigs thyme, leaves picked, optional

**Method**

1. Heat the oven to 200°C. Slice the potatoes 2 mm thick on a mandoline.
2. Do not rinse them. The surface starch is the glue that holds the cake together.
3. Brush a 22 cm ovenproof skillet heavily with clarified butter. Arrange the best slices in a tight overlapping spiral on the base, since this becomes the top when you turn it out.
4. Build up in layers, brushing each with butter and seasoning lightly. Press down as you go.
5. Set the pan on the hob over medium heat for 5 minutes until you hear it sizzling and the edges colour.
6. Cover with foil, bake 30 minutes, then uncover and bake 20 minutes more until the top is deep brown and a knife slides through with no resistance.
7. Rest 5 minutes, run a knife around the edge, and invert onto a plate in one confident movement.

**Cook's note:** Clarified butter matters here. Regular butter has milk solids that burn over the long bake and leave a bitter edge.

## 5. Croissant

A two-day project. The dough is laminated with a butter block, folded three times, and each fold traps more layers. Cold dough and cold butter, always.

**Makes 12 | Prep 2 hr across 2 days | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**

*Détrempe*
- 500 g strong bread flour
- 60 g sugar
- 11 g salt
- 10 g instant yeast
- 140 ml whole milk, cold
- 140 ml water, cold
- 40 g soft butter

*Butter block*
- 280 g high-fat European butter, cold

*Wash*
- 1 egg, beaten with a splash of milk

**Method**

1. Mix the détrempe ingredients into a shaggy dough, knead 5 minutes until it comes together, then flatten into a rectangle, wrap, and chill overnight.
2. Next day, pound the cold butter between two sheets of paper into a 20 cm square. It should bend without cracking, around 14°C.
3. Roll the dough to 40 by 20 cm, place the butter on one half, fold over, and seal the edges.
4. Roll out to about 60 by 20 cm, fold in thirds like a letter, wrap, and chill 45 minutes. Repeat twice more, always rolling in the same direction and always chilling between folds.
5. Roll the finished dough to 40 by 30 cm, 4 mm thick. Cut long triangles with a 10 cm base. Notch the base, stretch slightly, and roll from base to tip.
6. Set on lined trays, cover loosely, and prove at 24 to 26°C for 2 to 3 hours until visibly puffy and jiggly. Warmer than that and the butter leaks out.
7. Brush with egg wash and bake at 200°C for 8 minutes, then 180°C for 10 to 12 minutes until deep brown all over. Cool on a rack.

**Cook's note:** If butter leaks in the oven, the dough got too warm during folding or the prove was too hot. Both are fixable next time. The croissants will still taste good.

## 6. Crème Brûlée

Baked custard with a shattered sugar lid. The custard should barely hold, wobbling in the middle when you shake the ramekin.

**Serves 6 | Prep 15 min | Cook 40 min plus chilling**

**Ingredients**
- 500 ml heavy cream
- 1 vanilla pod, split and scraped
- 6 egg yolks
- 90 g caster sugar
- Pinch of salt
- 6 tbsp demerara or caster sugar, for the top

**Method**

1. Heat the cream with the vanilla pod and seeds until it steams. Take it off the heat and let it sit 20 minutes to infuse.
2. Whisk the yolks with the sugar and salt just until combined. Don't beat air into them.
3. Pour the warm cream over the yolks in a slow stream, whisking. Strain into a jug and skim off any foam.
4. Divide between six shallow ramekins and set them in a deep tray. Pour hot water halfway up the sides.
5. Bake at 150°C for 30 to 40 minutes. The edges should be set and the centres still loose.
6. Cool, then chill at least 4 hours or overnight.
7. Blot the surface dry, sprinkle an even thin layer of sugar, and blowtorch until it bubbles and turns amber. Wait 2 minutes for it to harden before serving.

**Cook's note:** Shallow ramekins beat deep ones. You get a better ratio of caramel to custard, and they bake more evenly.

## 7. Pain de Campagne

Country bread with a little rye and a natural starter. Long fermentation does the flavour work while you're asleep.

**Makes 1 large loaf | Prep 40 min across 2 days | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 400 g strong white flour
- 50 g wholemeal flour
- 50 g rye flour
- 375 ml water at 28°C
- 100 g active sourdough starter
- 10 g salt

**Method**

1. Mix the flours with 350 ml of the water and leave 45 minutes. This rest hydrates the flour and shortens kneading later.
2. Add the starter and squeeze it through the dough. Rest 30 minutes, then add the salt with the remaining 25 ml water and work it in.
3. Over the next 3 to 4 hours, do four sets of stretch and folds, spaced 45 minutes apart. The dough should get smoother and airier each time.
4. When it's risen by about 50 percent and feels alive, tip it out, shape it into a loose round, and rest 20 minutes.
5. Shape tightly into a boule, seam side up in a floured banneton, and refrigerate overnight, 12 to 16 hours.
6. Heat a Dutch oven at 250°C for 45 minutes. Turn the loaf out, score once decisively about 1 cm deep, and lower it in.
7. Bake covered 20 minutes, then uncovered at 220°C for 25 minutes until dark brown. Cool completely on a rack before cutting.

**Cook's note:** Cutting bread hot is a real temptation and it ruins the crumb. Give it two hours.

## 8. Filet de Bœuf en Croûte

Beef fillet in pastry with a mushroom duxelles. Wellington by another name. The enemy is moisture, so every stage is about drying things out.

**Serves 6 | Prep 1 hr plus chilling | Cook 40 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg centre-cut beef fillet, trimmed
- 500 g puff pastry, all butter
- 12 thin slices prosciutto or Bayonne ham
- 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 egg yolk beaten with 1 tbsp cream
- Oil, salt, pepper

*Duxelles*
- 700 g mushrooms, chestnut or a mix
- 3 shallots, 2 garlic cloves
- 50 g butter
- 100 ml Madeira or dry sherry
- 2 sprigs thyme

*Crêpes, optional but useful*
- 60 g flour, 1 egg, 150 ml milk, pinch of salt

**Method**

1. Blitz the mushrooms, shallots, and garlic to a coarse paste. Fry in butter over medium-high for 20 to 25 minutes until every drop of water has gone and it looks dark and dry. This takes longer than you expect.
2. Add the Madeira and thyme and cook until dry again. Season and cool completely.
3. Sear the salted fillet in a very hot pan for 90 seconds a side, all around. Cool, then brush with mustard.
4. Make two thin crêpes if using. Lay cling film on the bench, overlap the ham slices into a sheet, spread the duxelles over, add the crêpe, then the beef.
5. Roll it up tightly using the cling film, twist the ends like a sweet wrapper, and chill 30 minutes until firm.
6. Roll the pastry out to 4 mm. Unwrap the beef, sit it on the pastry, and wrap, trimming the overlap and sealing the seam underneath. Chill another 30 minutes.
7. Egg wash, score the top lightly, and bake at 210°C for 25 to 30 minutes until the internal temperature is 50°C. Rest 15 minutes before slicing thickly.

**Cook's note:** A soggy bottom comes from steam. The crêpe layer absorbs it, and so does baking on a preheated tray.

## 9. Steak au Poivre

Steak under a crust of cracked pepper with a cream and cognac sauce. Crush the peppercorns yourself, coarsely. Pre-ground pepper turns to dust and burns.

**Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 15 min**

**Ingredients**
- 2 sirloin or fillet steaks, 250 g each, 3 cm thick
- 3 tbsp black peppercorns, coarsely cracked and sieved of dust
- Salt
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 40 g butter
- 2 shallots, finely chopped
- 60 ml cognac
- 150 ml beef stock
- 120 ml heavy cream
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard

**Method**

1. Take the steaks out of the fridge 40 minutes early. Salt them well.
2. Press the cracked pepper firmly onto both flat sides. Press, don't sprinkle.
3. Heat the oil in a heavy pan until it just begins to smoke. Lay the steaks in and don't move them for 3 minutes.
4. Flip, add half the butter, and cook 3 minutes more for medium rare, basting. Move to a plate to rest.
5. Lower the heat, add the shallots to the pan, and soften 2 minutes.
6. Take the pan off the flame, add the cognac, and return it to the heat. It may flame briefly. Let it reduce almost to nothing.
7. Add the stock, reduce by half, then the cream and mustard. Simmer until it coats a spoon, whisk in the remaining butter, and pour over the rested steaks along with any juices from the plate.

**Cook's note:** Sieve the cracked pepper. The fine dust burns in the pan and makes the sauce bitter, while the coarse pieces give you that crunch.

## 10. Magret de Canard

Duck breast from a fattened bird, cooked from a cold pan so the fat renders slowly. Served pink, with something sharp alongside.

**Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 2 duck breasts, 350 g each
- Salt and pepper

*Sauce*
- 2 shallots, chopped
- 150 ml red wine
- 100 ml duck or chicken stock
- 2 tbsp honey
- 1 tbsp sherry vinegar
- 100 g cherries or blackcurrants, optional
- 20 g cold butter

**Method**

1. Score the skin in a diamond pattern, cutting through the fat but not into the meat. Season heavily with salt.
2. Lay the breasts skin side down in a cold, dry pan. Turn the heat to medium-low.
3. Let them render for 12 to 15 minutes. Pour off the fat two or three times as it collects, and save it for potatoes. The skin should end up thin, flat, and deep gold.
4. Flip and cook 3 minutes on the flesh side. Move to a board and rest 10 minutes, skin side up.
5. Pour off all but a spoonful of fat. Soften the shallots, then add the wine and reduce to a syrup.
6. Add the stock, honey, vinegar, and fruit. Simmer until it thickens.
7. Off the heat, swirl in the cold butter. Slice the duck thinly across the grain, pour the sauce around rather than over, and serve.

**Cook's note:** Starting in a cold pan feels wrong and is correct. A hot pan seizes the skin before the fat underneath can melt out.

## 11. Baguette

Four ingredients, and one of the harder things in this book. The flavour comes from a poolish left overnight.

**Makes 3 | Prep 30 min plus overnight | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**

*Poolish*
- 200 g strong white flour
- 200 ml water
- 0.5 g instant yeast (a small pinch)

*Final dough*
- 300 g strong white flour
- 140 ml water
- 3 g instant yeast
- 11 g salt

**Method**

1. Whisk the poolish ingredients, cover, and leave at room temperature 12 to 16 hours. It's ready when domed and just starting to fall in the centre.
2. Mix the poolish with the remaining flour, water, and yeast. Rest 30 minutes, then add the salt and knead 6 minutes until smooth.
3. Bulk ferment 2 hours, folding the dough over itself every 40 minutes.
4. Divide into three, pre-shape into loose logs, and rest 20 minutes under a cloth.
5. Shape each into a 35 cm baguette: flatten gently, fold the top third down, the bottom third up, seal along the seam with the heel of your hand, then roll to length.
6. Prove seam side up in a floured couche for 60 to 75 minutes.
7. Flip onto a peel, slash at a shallow angle with 4 or 5 overlapping cuts, and bake at 250°C with plenty of steam for 12 minutes, then 230°C without steam for 10 minutes until dark gold and crackling as it cools.

**Cook's note:** For steam at home, put a metal tray on the oven floor while preheating and throw a cup of boiling water into it as the bread goes in. Stand back.

## 12. Gratin Dauphinois

Sliced potatoes baked in cream and milk with garlic. No cheese in the traditional version, though nobody will stop you. The starch thickens the cream, which is why you don't rinse the slices.

**Serves 6 | Prep 20 min | Cook 1½ hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1.4 kg floury potatoes, peeled and sliced 3 mm
- 400 ml whole milk
- 400 ml heavy cream
- 2 garlic cloves, one halved and one crushed
- Nutmeg, freshly grated
- 30 g butter
- Salt and white pepper

**Method**

1. Rub a wide gratin dish with the cut garlic clove, then butter it well.
2. Put the milk, cream, crushed garlic, plenty of salt, pepper, and a good grating of nutmeg into a wide pan.
3. Add the potato slices and bring to a bare simmer. Cook 8 minutes, stirring gently so nothing sticks. The liquid will thicken noticeably.
4. Tip everything into the dish and level the top, pressing the potatoes down so the liquid comes almost to the surface.
5. Bake at 160°C for 60 to 75 minutes. Low and slow is right here.
6. It's done when the top is browned in patches and a knife meets no resistance.
7. Rest 15 minutes before serving. It sets slightly and cuts far better.

**Cook's note:** Floury potatoes, not waxy. You want them to give up starch and half-collapse into the cream.

## 13. Canapés au Saumon Fumé

Smoked salmon on something crisp with something creamy. Simple, so the details are all you have: good salmon, cold butter, sharp knife.

**Makes 24 | Prep 25 min | Cook 10 min**

**Ingredients**
- 300 g cold-smoked salmon, sliced
- 12 slices dense rye or pain de mie
- 150 g crème fraîche
- 80 g cream cheese
- 2 tbsp finely chopped dill, plus sprigs
- 1 tbsp horseradish, or 2 tsp Dijon
- Zest and juice of 1 lemon
- 2 tbsp capers, rinsed
- ½ red onion, sliced paper thin
- Black pepper, olive oil

**Method**

1. Beat the crème fraîche, cream cheese, chopped dill, horseradish, lemon zest, and a squeeze of juice until smooth. Season and chill.
2. Cut the crusts off the bread and slice each piece into two rectangles or rounds.
3. Brush lightly with olive oil and bake at 180°C for 8 to 10 minutes until crisp at the edges but not brittle. Cool.
4. Soak the onion slices in cold water with a splash of lemon for 10 minutes, then drain. This takes the raw bite out.
5. Pipe or spoon the cream onto each base.
6. Drape a folded ribbon of salmon on top. Folding gives height and looks better than lying it flat.
7. Finish with capers, onion, a dill sprig, black pepper, and a tiny squeeze of lemon just before serving.

**Cook's note:** Assemble within an hour of serving. The bases go soft after that and there's no rescuing them.

## 14. Soupe à l'Oignon Gratinée

Onions cooked down for the better part of an hour, beef stock, bread, and a lid of melted cheese. The colour of the onions determines everything.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 1½ hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg onions, halved and sliced thin
- 50 g butter
- 1 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp sugar
- 2 tbsp plain flour
- 150 ml dry white wine
- 1.5 litres good beef stock
- 2 bay leaves, 3 sprigs thyme
- 1 tbsp brandy, optional
- 8 slices baguette
- 200 g Gruyère or Comté, grated
- Salt, pepper

**Method**

1. Melt the butter with the oil in a wide heavy pot. Add the onions and a good pinch of salt, cover, and sweat 15 minutes.
2. Uncover, add the sugar, and cook over medium heat for 45 minutes, stirring every few minutes. They'll go from pale to gold to deep mahogany. If they stick, add a splash of water and scrape.
3. Stir in the flour and cook 2 minutes.
4. Add the wine, scraping the base, and reduce by half.
5. Pour in the stock with the herbs. Simmer 30 minutes, then season and add the brandy.
6. Toast the baguette slices until dry. Ladle the soup into ovenproof bowls, float the bread on top, and heap the cheese over so it covers the rim.
7. Grill until the cheese is bubbling and browned in patches. Let it sit 2 minutes so nobody burns their mouth.

**Cook's note:** The 45 minutes of caramelising is the recipe. There's no shortcut, and pale onions give you a thin, sweet soup with no depth.

## 15. Sole Meunière

Flour, butter, lemon, parsley. Four steps and one pan. It's the dish that made Julia Child change careers, and it takes about eight minutes.

**Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 10 min**

**Ingredients**
- 2 whole Dover sole, 400 g each, skinned and trimmed, or 4 fillets
- 60 g plain flour
- Salt and white pepper
- 2 tbsp clarified butter or oil, for frying
- 80 g cold unsalted butter, cubed
- 1 lemon, half juiced, half cut into wedges
- 2 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, chopped

**Method**

1. Pat the fish bone dry. Season both sides.
2. Season the flour and dredge the fish, pressing lightly, then shake off every bit of loose flour. Excess flour burns and clouds the butter.
3. Heat the clarified butter in a large pan over medium-high until shimmering.
4. Lay the fish in, presentation side down. Cook 4 minutes without touching it.
5. Turn carefully with two spatulas and cook 3 minutes more. Slide onto warm plates.
6. Wipe the pan, add the cold butter, and swirl over medium heat until it foams and the solids turn nut brown and smell like toffee. Roughly 90 seconds.
7. Take it off the heat immediately, add the lemon juice (it will spit), stir in the parsley, and pour over the fish. Serve with lemon wedges and boiled potatoes.

**Cook's note:** Beurre noisette goes from perfect to burnt in about ten seconds. Have your lemon juice measured and standing by before you start browning.
