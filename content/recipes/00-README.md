## How to read a recipe here

Every recipe follows the same shape:

- **One or two lines of context.** What the dish actually is, and occasionally why I think one version beats another.
- **Serves / Prep / Cook.** Rough numbers. Your stove is not my stove.
- **Ingredients.** Grouped when a recipe has separate components (spice paste, sauce, garnish).
- **Method.** Numbered, in the order you'd actually do them.
- **Cook's note.** The one thing that usually goes wrong, or a swap for something hard to find.

## A few conventions

- Measurements are metric with cups and spoons alongside. 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 tsp = 5 ml, 1 cup = 240 ml.
- "Neutral oil" means sunflower, canola, rice bran, or plain vegetable oil. Not olive oil unless the recipe says so.
- Oven temperatures are for a conventional oven. If yours is fan-forced, drop it by about 20°C (35°F).
- Salt amounts assume fine sea salt. Kosher salt is fluffier, so you need a bit more by volume.
- When a recipe says "taste it," that isn't filler. Chillies, fish sauce, and tamarind vary wildly between brands.

## Shopping notes by section

**Indonesian and Thai** lean hard on fresh aromatics: galangal, lemongrass, kaffir lime leaf, candlenut. An Asian grocer will have all of it, usually frozen. Frozen galangal is fine. Dried galangal is not.

**Japanese** needs a small pantry investment up front: soy sauce, mirin, sake, dashi (instant granules are respectable), rice vinegar. After that, most recipes cost very little.

**French** is mostly technique, not ingredients. Good butter matters more than anything you'll buy.

**Arabian Peninsula** recipes use loomi (dried lime), baharat, and rose or orange blossom water. Loomi has no substitute worth using.

**African** recipes here span a continent, so the pantries differ: berbere and niter kibbeh for the Ethiopian dish, suya spice for the Nigerian ones, harissa for North Africa.

## Substitutions I'll allow without complaint

- Palm sugar → dark brown sugar
- Kecap manis → soy sauce plus an equal amount of dark brown sugar, simmered a minute
- Tamarind paste → lime juice plus a pinch of brown sugar (not identical, but it works)
- Crème fraîche → sour cream with a spoonful of heavy cream stirred in
- Sake → dry sherry, or water plus a splash of rice vinegar

## Substitutions that will disappoint you

- Ground spices in place of a wet spice paste. The paste is the dish.
- Lime zest for kaffir lime leaf.
- Pre-ground pepper in steak au poivre.
- Regular short-grain rice for glutinous rice in mango sticky rice.

Cook in whatever order you like. The numbering is for finding things, not for ranking them.
