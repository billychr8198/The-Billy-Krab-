# Arabian Peninsula & Levant Food

This chapter covers the Gulf, Yemen, the Hejaz, and the Levant, which is a lot of ground. What holds it together is a shared pantry: cardamom, dried lime, saffron, rose water, dates, ghee, and rice cooked with attention.

A few things worth buying before you start:

**Loomi** (dried black lime) shows up in half the savoury dishes here. Pierce it before adding it whole, or grind it to powder. There's no substitute that behaves the same way.

**Baharat** varies by country and even by household. A workable Gulf blend: 2 parts black pepper, 2 parts cumin, 1 part coriander, 1 part cinnamon, 1 part clove, 1 part cardamom, 1 part nutmeg, plus dried rose petals if you have them.

**Ghee** is not interchangeable with butter in these recipes. The water content in butter changes how rice cooks.

One honest note: laddu is Indian, not Arabian. It's in this chapter because it's genuinely everywhere in Oman, the UAE, and the eastern Gulf, sold in the same shops as halwa and eaten at the same occasions. Centuries of Indian Ocean trade did that.

## 1. Shawarma

Stacked marinated meat cooked on a vertical spit. At home, the honest version is a tightly packed loaf roasted in the oven and sliced thin, which gets you surprisingly close.

**Serves 6 | Prep 30 min plus overnight | Cook 1½ hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg boneless chicken thigh, skin on, or lamb shoulder sliced thin
- 200 g extra chicken skin or lamb fat

*Marinade*
- 4 tbsp yoghurt
- 3 tbsp lemon juice
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 6 garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 tbsp baharat
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp paprika
- ½ tsp ground cardamom
- ½ tsp cinnamon
- 2 tsp salt
- 1 tbsp white vinegar

*To serve*
- Flatbread, toum (garlic sauce), tahini sauce, pickled turnips, tomato, parsley, sumac onions

**Method**

1. Slice the chicken into flat pieces of even thickness. Mix the marinade and rub it through. Refrigerate overnight.
2. Line a loaf tin with foil. Layer the meat in flat, alternating direction, with pieces of fat or skin every few layers. Press each layer down hard.
3. Fold the foil over, weigh it down with something heavy, and refrigerate 2 hours so it compacts.
4. Roast at 200°C for 40 minutes covered, then uncover and roast 30 minutes more until the top is dark and crusted.
5. Rest 20 minutes, then chill for an hour if you can. Cold meat slices far thinner.
6. Slice as thinly as your knife allows, then crisp the slices in a hot dry pan for 90 seconds.
7. Fill warm flatbread with meat, sauces, pickles, and onions. Roll tight and press on a hot pan to seal.

**Cook's note:** The fat layers aren't optional. Lean shawarma turns dry and stringy, and the rendered fat is what crisps the edges.

## 2. Kunafa Nabulsiyeh

Shredded pastry over stretchy cheese, soaked in syrup. The Nablus version uses a mild white cheese that has been soaked to draw out the salt. It should pull into strings when you cut it.

**Serves 10 | Prep 25 min | Cook 40 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g kataifi (shredded phyllo), thawed
- 250 g ghee, melted
- 700 g Nabulsi or Akkawi cheese, soaked, plus 200 g low-moisture mozzarella
- Orange food colouring, traditional but optional
- 100 g crushed pistachios

*Syrup*
- 400 g sugar
- 250 ml water
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tbsp rose water
- 1 tbsp orange blossom water

**Method**

1. Soak the cheese in cold water for 4 to 6 hours, changing the water four times. Taste a piece: it should be mildly salty, not briny. Grate or slice it and drain well.
2. Make the syrup first. Boil the sugar and water for 8 minutes, add lemon juice, then take off the heat and stir in the flower waters. Cool it completely, because cold syrup on hot kunafa is the rule.
3. Pull the kataifi apart with your fingers and chop it into 1 cm lengths.
4. Work the melted ghee through it with your hands until every strand is coated and it looks slightly damp.
5. Press half the pastry firmly into a buttered 30 cm round pan. Press hard, all the way to the edges.
6. Spread the cheese over, leaving a 1 cm border, then cover with the rest of the pastry and press again.
7. Bake at 200°C for 35 to 40 minutes until the edges are deep gold. Invert onto a tray immediately, pour the cold syrup over while it's still hot, and scatter pistachios. Serve within the hour.

**Cook's note:** Cold syrup, hot kunafa. Reverse it and the pastry goes soggy instead of absorbing and staying crisp.

## 3. Qatayef

Small stuffed pancakes, cooked on one side only so the top stays open and spongy. Ramadan food, filled with either walnuts or sweet cheese, then fried or baked and soaked.

**Makes 24 | Prep 20 min plus 1 hr rest | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**

*Batter*
- 200 g plain flour
- 50 g fine semolina
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp instant yeast
- ½ tsp baking powder
- ¼ tsp salt
- 450 ml warm water

*Fillings*
- Walnut: 200 g walnuts, chopped, 2 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp cinnamon, 1 tsp orange blossom water
- Cheese: 300 g ashta or ricotta mixed with soaked Akkawi

*Syrup and finishing*
- 300 g sugar, 180 ml water, 1 tsp lemon juice, 1 tbsp rose water
- Oil for frying, crushed pistachios

**Method**

1. Blend all the batter ingredients until completely smooth. It should be thinner than pancake batter, closer to double cream. Rest 1 hour until bubbly.
2. Make the syrup and cool it.
3. Heat a non-stick pan over medium with no oil. Pour in 2 tbsp of batter and let it spread into a 9 cm circle.
4. Cook one side only. Bubbles will rise and burst across the surface. When the top looks completely dry and matte, about 90 seconds, lift it off. Never flip it.
5. Cool the pancakes on a cloth, uncovered, so they don't steam and go slippery.
6. Put a spoonful of filling on the cooked side, fold in half, and pinch the edge closed from one end to the other. It should seal with just pressure.
7. Fry at 175°C for 2 minutes until golden, or bake at 200°C with a brush of ghee for 15 minutes. Dip straight into cold syrup, then pistachios.

**Cook's note:** If the edges won't seal, the pancakes have dried out too long. Cover them with a slightly damp cloth for a few minutes and try again.

## 4. Mansaf

Jordan's national dish: lamb cooked in fermented dried yoghurt called jameed, served over rice and flatbread. Eaten communally from one platter, traditionally with the right hand.

**Serves 8 | Prep 30 min plus soaking | Cook 3 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 2 kg lamb shoulder or leg, on the bone, in large pieces
- 500 g jameed, broken and soaked overnight in 1.5 litres water
- 2 onions, quartered
- 3 bay leaves, 6 cardamom pods, 2 cinnamon sticks, 1 tbsp black peppercorns
- 2 tsp turmeric
- 1 tbsp baharat
- 100 g ghee
- 500 g long-grain rice
- 2 large sheets shrak or markook bread
- 150 g toasted almonds and pine nuts
- Parsley, salt

**Method**

1. Blend the soaked jameed with its water until smooth, then strain through a sieve. Set aside.
2. Brown the lamb in ghee in batches. Add the onions, whole spices, turmeric, and baharat, then cover with water and simmer 2 hours until tender. Skim regularly.
3. Lift the lamb out. Strain and reserve 500 ml of the cooking broth.
4. Heat the jameed liquid in a large pot, stirring constantly in one direction until it comes to a bare simmer. It must not boil or it will split.
5. Add the lamb and the reserved broth and simmer very gently for 30 minutes. Season.
6. Cook the rice with ghee, a pinch of turmeric, and salt.
7. Line a large platter with the bread, ladle some jameed sauce over to soften it, then rice, then lamb on top. Pour more sauce over, scatter nuts and parsley, and serve the remaining sauce in bowls.

**Cook's note:** Stirring in one direction while the jameed heats is a real technique, not folklore. It keeps the yoghurt proteins from separating.

## 5. Dajaj Mashwi

Grilled chicken with garlic, lemon, and spice, served with toum. It's what a Levantine rotisserie shop sells all day, and the marinade does most of the work.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min plus 6 hr | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 chicken, 1.6 kg, spatchcocked, or 8 pieces
- 6 garlic cloves, crushed
- 4 tbsp lemon juice
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- 3 tbsp yoghurt
- 1 tbsp baharat
- 2 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp cumin
- 1 tsp sumac
- ½ tsp cinnamon
- 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper

*Toum*
- 1 head garlic, peeled and green germs removed
- 1 tsp salt
- 400 ml neutral oil
- 3 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp ice water

**Method**

1. Slash the chicken through the thickest parts of the thigh and breast. Rub the marinade in everywhere and refrigerate 6 hours or overnight.
2. For toum, blend the garlic and salt to a paste, scraping down twice.
3. With the machine running, add the oil in a slow thread, alternating with the lemon juice. Start with literal drops. Adding oil too fast is the only way this fails.
4. Finish with the ice water to set it. It should be white, thick, and stiff enough to hold a peak.
5. Set up a grill with two zones. Cook the chicken skin side up on the indirect side, covered, for 30 minutes.
6. Move it over the coals skin side down for 8 minutes, watching for flare-ups from the oil.
7. Rest 10 minutes. Serve with toum, flatbread, pickles, and a sumac onion salad.

**Cook's note:** If the toum splits, put a tablespoon of the broken mixture in a clean bowl with a spoonful of ice water and slowly beat the rest back into it.

## 6. Laddu

Chickpea flour balls with ghee, sugar, and cardamom. Indian in origin and completely at home in Muscat and Dubai, where it's sold beside halwa in the same sweet shops.

**Makes 20 | Prep 15 min | Cook 30 min**

**Ingredients**
- 400 g besan (coarse chickpea flour)
- 200 g ghee, plus 2 tbsp
- 250 g caster sugar, ground fine
- 1½ tsp ground cardamom
- 3 tbsp chopped cashews and almonds
- 2 tbsp raisins
- Generous pinch saffron, soaked in 1 tbsp warm milk
- 2 tbsp melon seeds, optional

**Method**

1. Fry the nuts and raisins in 2 tbsp ghee until golden. Remove and set aside.
2. Melt the remaining ghee in a heavy pan over low heat and add the besan.
3. Roast it, stirring constantly, for 20 to 25 minutes. It goes from pale yellow to sandy to golden brown, and the smell changes completely, from raw and dusty to nutty. That smell is the only reliable signal.
4. Take it off the heat and let it cool to just above body temperature. Too hot and the sugar melts into syrup.
5. Stir in the sugar, cardamom, saffron milk, nuts, and raisins.
6. Squeeze a handful in your palm to test. If it holds, start rolling. If it crumbles, add a tablespoon of warm ghee.
7. Roll into balls about 40 g each, pressing firmly. Cool completely on a tray. They firm up as they sit and keep two weeks in a tin.

**Cook's note:** Low heat and constant stirring for the full 25 minutes. Under-roasted besan tastes raw and chalky, and no amount of sugar hides it.

## 7. Sayadiyah

Fish with rice cooked in caramelised onion stock. The onions are cooked far past golden, almost to the edge of burnt, and that darkness is the entire flavour and colour of the dish.

**Serves 6 | Prep 30 min | Cook 1 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg firm white fish, hamour, grouper, or sea bass, in thick steaks
- 500 g basmati rice, soaked 30 min
- 5 large onions, sliced thin
- 120 ml olive oil
- 2 tsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander, 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp allspice
- 1 tbsp baharat
- 900 ml water or fish stock
- 100 g toasted pine nuts and almonds
- Salt, pepper, lemon

*Tahini sauce*
- 150 g tahini, juice of 2 lemons, 2 garlic cloves crushed, water to loosen, salt

**Method**

1. Season the fish with salt, half the cumin, and lemon. Rest 20 minutes.
2. Fry the onions in the oil over medium heat for 30 to 35 minutes. Keep going past golden until they're a deep coffee brown, but stop before anything turns black and bitter.
3. Lift out a third of the onions and drain them for the garnish.
4. Add the remaining spices to the pot, cook 1 minute, then add the water. Simmer 10 minutes and blend, or push through a sieve, into a dark savoury stock.
5. Fry the fish in a separate pan until browned on both sides but not cooked through. Set aside.
6. Drain the rice and add it to the stock in a ratio of about 1 part rice to 1.5 parts liquid. Bring to a boil, cover, and cook 15 minutes on the lowest heat.
7. Lay the fish on top for the last 8 minutes so it steams through. Rest 10 minutes, then serve topped with the reserved onions, nuts, and tahini sauce.

**Cook's note:** Judging the onions is the skill. Pull one out and taste it: sweet and deeply savoury is right, sharply bitter means you went too far and the whole dish will taste of it.

## 8. Ma'amoul

Semolina shortbread stuffed with dates, walnuts, or pistachios, pressed in carved wooden moulds. Made in large batches before Eid and Easter.

**Makes 40 | Prep 40 min plus overnight rest | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**

*Dough*
- 500 g fine semolina
- 100 g coarse semolina
- 100 g plain flour
- 300 g ghee, melted
- 60 g sugar
- 1 tsp instant yeast
- 1 tbsp mahlab, optional
- 120 ml milk, warm
- 2 tbsp rose water
- 2 tbsp orange blossom water

*Fillings*
- Date: 400 g date paste worked with 2 tbsp ghee and 1 tsp cinnamon
- Walnut: 300 g walnuts, 80 g sugar, 1 tbsp orange blossom water, 1 tsp cinnamon
- Pistachio: 300 g pistachios, 80 g sugar, 1 tbsp rose water

**Method**

1. Rub the melted ghee into the semolinas and flour with your hands until every grain is coated. Cover and leave overnight at room temperature. This rest is what makes the texture sandy rather than hard.
2. Next day, dissolve the yeast and sugar in the warm milk and add it with the flower waters and mahlab. Knead lightly, just until it holds. Overworking makes them tough.
3. Rest 30 minutes. Roll each filling into small balls, about 10 g each.
4. Take 25 g of dough, roll it into a ball, and press your thumb into the centre to make a cup.
5. Put a filling ball in and pinch the dough closed over it, then roll smooth.
6. Press into a floured ma'amoul mould, then knock it out firmly onto a lined tray. If you don't have moulds, flatten slightly and decorate with a fork or tweezers.
7. Bake at 170°C for 15 to 18 minutes. They should stay pale, with only the base coloured. Cool completely, then dust the nut ones with icing sugar.

**Cook's note:** Browned ma'amoul are overbaked. Pull them out while they still look underdone, because they firm up considerably as they cool.

## 9. Ogaily (Ageeli)

A Bahraini saffron and cardamom cake, topped with sesame and nigella seeds. Dense, fragrant, and served with tea. It's sometimes just called Bahraini cake.

**Serves 10 | Prep 15 min | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 300 g plain flour
- 100 g fine semolina
- 300 g sugar
- 4 eggs
- 200 ml neutral oil
- 200 ml milk or water
- 2 tsp baking powder
- 2 tsp ground cardamom
- 1 tsp saffron threads, ground and soaked in 3 tbsp warm rose water
- ½ tsp turmeric, for colour
- ¼ tsp salt
- 3 tbsp sesame seeds
- 1 tbsp nigella seeds
- 2 tbsp slivered pistachios

**Method**

1. Grind the saffron with a pinch of sugar in a mortar, then soak in the warm rose water for 15 minutes. Grinding first extracts far more colour.
2. Beat the eggs and sugar for 5 minutes until pale and thickened.
3. Pour in the oil in a steady stream while beating, then the milk and the saffron water.
4. Sift in the flour, semolina, baking powder, cardamom, turmeric, and salt. Fold until just combined and no streaks remain.
5. Pour into a lined 24 cm tin. Let it stand 10 minutes so the semolina hydrates.
6. Scatter the sesame, nigella, and pistachios generously over the surface.
7. Bake at 175°C for 40 to 45 minutes until a skewer comes out clean. Cool in the tin 15 minutes, then turn out. Better on day two.

**Cook's note:** Real saffron is expensive and this cake uses a lot of it. The turmeric is there to carry the colour so you can use a lighter hand with the saffron without ending up with a pale cake.

## 10. Shuraik

A sweet enriched bread ring, crusted with sesame and nigella seeds. Eaten across the Hejaz and Sudan, especially at Eid, and it keeps well for a few days.

**Makes 8 rings | Prep 30 min plus 2 hr rising | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 600 g plain flour
- 100 g sugar
- 8 g instant yeast
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 eggs
- 100 g melted ghee or butter
- 250 ml warm milk
- 1 tsp ground cardamom
- ½ tsp ground fennel or anise, optional
- 1 tbsp rose water

*Topping*
- 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp milk
- 4 tbsp sesame seeds
- 1 tbsp nigella seeds

**Method**

1. Whisk the flour, sugar, yeast, salt, cardamom, and fennel together.
2. Add the eggs, ghee, rose water, and warm milk. Mix into a soft dough.
3. Knead 10 minutes until smooth and slightly tacky. It should pull away from the bowl but still stick lightly to your fingers.
4. Cover and rise 90 minutes until doubled.
5. Divide into 8. Roll each into a rope about 30 cm long, then join the ends into a ring, pressing the join firmly.
6. Set on lined trays, cover, and prove 40 minutes until puffy.
7. Brush with egg wash, press sesame and nigella seeds over the top, and bake at 180°C for 18 to 20 minutes until deep golden. Cool on a rack.

**Cook's note:** Press the seeds down with your palm after scattering them. If you just sprinkle, most end up on the tray.

## 11. Muhammar

Bahraini sweet rice with dates and date syrup, served with fried or grilled fish. The sweet and savoury combination sounds odd and works completely.

**Serves 6 | Prep 20 min plus soaking | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g basmati rice, soaked 1 hour
- 200 ml date syrup (dibs)
- 100 g soft dates, pitted and chopped
- 80 g ghee
- 1 tsp saffron, soaked in 3 tbsp rose water
- 1 tsp ground cardamom
- 4 cardamom pods, 1 cinnamon stick
- 2 tsp sugar
- 1½ tsp salt
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame seeds

*To serve*
- 6 fish steaks, salted, dusted in turmeric and flour, and fried

**Method**

1. Boil a large pot of salted water with the cardamom pods and cinnamon stick.
2. Drain the soaked rice and boil it for 5 to 6 minutes only, until the outside is soft and the centre is still hard. Drain immediately.
3. Warm the date syrup with the ghee, chopped dates, sugar, and ground cardamom until loose and pourable.
4. Spread a third of the date mixture in the base of a heavy pot with a little ghee.
5. Layer in the rice, drizzling the rest of the date mixture and the saffron rose water between layers. Don't stir it in, or the whole pot turns uniformly brown.
6. Poke a few holes down through the rice with a spoon handle, cover the pot with a cloth-wrapped lid, and cook on the lowest heat for 30 minutes.
7. Turn out so the caramelised bottom layer ends up on top. Scatter sesame seeds and serve with the fried fish alongside.

**Cook's note:** A cloth under the lid catches condensation. Without it the water drips back down and you lose the crust at the bottom.

## 12. Aseedah

A smooth cooked wheat dough, served in a mound with a well of butter and honey or date syrup in the middle. Eaten across Yemen, Oman, and Sudan, and it's a workout to make.

**Serves 4 | Prep 5 min | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**
- 300 g plain wheat flour, or a mix of wheat and barley
- 750 ml water
- 1 tsp salt

*To serve*
- 100 g ghee or samn
- 150 ml honey, or date syrup
- Ground cardamom
- Warm milk, optional

**Method**

1. Bring 600 ml of the water to a boil with the salt.
2. Whisk the flour into the remaining 150 ml of cold water into a smooth slurry with no lumps.
3. Pour the slurry into the boiling water while whisking hard. It'll thicken fast.
4. Reduce the heat to low. Now switch to a sturdy wooden spoon and beat the dough continuously for 15 to 20 minutes, pressing it against the sides of the pot and folding it over.
5. It's done when it pulls away from the sides cleanly and comes together as one smooth glossy mass with no raw flour taste.
6. Wet a bowl, press the dough in, then turn it out onto a plate to make a dome. Make a deep well in the top with the back of a wet spoon.
7. Fill the well with melted ghee and honey, dust with cardamom, and eat from the outside in with your fingers, dipping each piece.

**Cook's note:** Your arm will hurt. That's the recipe working. Stopping early leaves you with lumpy paste that tastes of raw flour.

## 13. Saloona

The everyday Gulf stew: meat or fish with vegetables, tomato, loomi, and baharat, served over rice. Every household has a slightly different version and none of them are wrong.

**Serves 6 | Prep 20 min | Cook 1½ hr**

**Ingredients**
- 800 g lamb shoulder or chicken thigh, in chunks
- 2 onions, chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, 3 cm ginger, made into a paste
- 3 tomatoes, chopped, plus 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 potatoes, 2 carrots, 1 courgette, 1 aubergine, all in chunks
- 2 loomi, pierced, or 1 tsp loomi powder
- 1 tbsp baharat
- 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cumin, ½ tsp cinnamon
- 2 green chillies, split
- 1.2 litres stock or water
- 3 tbsp ghee or oil
- Coriander, salt
- Rice to serve

**Method**

1. Brown the meat in ghee in a heavy pot, in batches so it colours rather than steams.
2. Add the onions and cook 8 minutes until soft and golden.
3. Add the garlic and ginger paste, then the spices, and fry 90 seconds until the smell turns from raw to toasty.
4. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 2 minutes, then the fresh tomatoes until they break down.
5. Return the meat with the stock, loomi, and chillies. Simmer covered for 1 hour.
6. Add the root vegetables and cook 15 minutes, then the courgette and aubergine for a final 12 minutes so they don't disintegrate.
7. Fish out the loomi if you like, since they get bitter if left too long. Season, scatter coriander, and serve over rice.

**Cook's note:** Adding the vegetables in stages instead of all at once is the difference between a stew and a mush. Hardest first, softest last.

## 14. Mufattah

Spiced rice with fish, built over a base of thin flatbread that soaks up everything that runs down. A Gulf coastal dish, plain in principle and generous in practice.

**Serves 6 | Prep 30 min | Cook 1 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg firm white fish, hamour or snapper, in steaks
- 500 g basmati rice, soaked 30 min
- 2 large sheets thin flatbread (raqaq or markook)
- 3 onions, sliced
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 tomatoes, chopped
- 2 loomi, pierced
- 1 tbsp baharat, 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander
- 1 tsp saffron in 3 tbsp rose water
- 80 g ghee
- 900 ml fish stock or water
- 60 g raisins and 60 g toasted almonds
- Salt, lemon, coriander

**Method**

1. Season the fish with salt, turmeric, and lemon. Rest 20 minutes, then fry in ghee until browned on both sides. Set aside without cooking it through.
2. In the same pot, fry the onions for 15 minutes until deep golden.
3. Add the garlic and dry spices, cook 1 minute, then the tomatoes and loomi. Cook until pulpy.
4. Add the stock and simmer 10 minutes to build a flavoured broth. Taste and salt it well.
5. Drain the rice, add it to the broth, bring to a boil, then cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 minutes.
6. Drizzle the saffron rose water over the surface, lay the fish on top, cover, and steam 10 minutes more.
7. Line a large platter with the flatbread, spoon the rice over it, arrange the fish on top, and finish with raisins, almonds, and coriander. Serve lemon on the side.

**Cook's note:** Tear the bread rather than cutting it, and let it sit under the rice for a few minutes before serving so it softens without turning to paste.

## 15. Fatoot

Yemeni torn flatbread fried with eggs, tomato, and chilli. Breakfast, or dinner when you have leftover bread and nothing else. It takes ten minutes.

**Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 12 min**

**Ingredients**
- 3 stale flatbreads, or 1 large khubz, torn into rough pieces
- 4 eggs
- 3 tbsp ghee
- 1 onion, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, chopped
- 2 tomatoes, chopped
- 2 green chillies, chopped
- 1 tsp cumin
- ½ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp hawaij spice mix, if you have it
- Salt, pepper
- Coriander
- Zhoug (green chilli relish) to serve

**Method**

1. Tear the bread into pieces roughly the size of a large coin. Stale is better than fresh, because it holds shape instead of dissolving.
2. Melt the ghee and fry the bread pieces for 4 minutes until they crisp at the edges. Lift them out.
3. Add the onion to the pan and cook 4 minutes, then the garlic and chillies for 1 minute.
4. Add the tomatoes and spices and cook until they collapse into a rough sauce.
5. Return the bread and toss so it soaks up some of the sauce but keeps a bit of texture.
6. Beat the eggs, season them, and pour over. Fold gently with a spatula until just set, still glossy.
7. Take it off the heat while it looks slightly underdone, since it keeps cooking in the pan. Coriander over the top, zhoug on the side.

**Cook's note:** Frying the bread separately first is worth the extra step. Add it raw and the whole thing turns into a soggy scramble.
