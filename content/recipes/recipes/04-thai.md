# Thai Food

Thai cooking is a balancing act between salty, sour, sweet, and hot. Not equal parts, and not the same balance in every dish. A northeastern salad leans sour and salty. A central Thai curry leans sweet and rich. Bangkok street noodles lean sweet.

Which means: taste constantly and adjust at the end. Fish sauce brands vary in saltiness by a wide margin, limes vary in acidity, and palm sugar varies in sweetness depending on how it was made. A recipe gives you a starting point, not a destination.

Buy a granite mortar and pestle if you cook Thai food more than occasionally. Pounding bruises and releases oils in a way that blending doesn't, and the difference in a curry paste is obvious.

## 1. Phanaeng Curry

Thicker and sweeter than red curry, with ground peanuts in the paste and very little liquid. The sauce should cling to the meat rather than pool around it.

**Serves 4 | Prep 30 min | Cook 30 min**

**Ingredients**

*Paste*
- 10 dried long red chillies, seeded and soaked
- 1 tsp coriander seed, 1 tsp cumin seed, both toasted
- 2 tbsp chopped lemongrass
- 1 tbsp chopped galangal
- 1 tbsp chopped coriander root
- 1 tbsp chopped kaffir lime zest
- 6 shallots, 6 garlic cloves
- 1 tsp shrimp paste
- 3 tbsp roasted peanuts, ground
- 1 tsp salt

*Curry*
- 600 g beef sirloin or chicken thigh, sliced thin
- 400 ml thick coconut milk (don't shake the can)
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- 1½ tbsp palm sugar
- 5 kaffir lime leaves, 3 shredded
- 2 red chillies, sliced lengthwise
- Thai basil

**Method**

1. Pound the paste in order: hardest first. Chillies and salt, then lemongrass, galangal, coriander root, lime zest, then shallot and garlic, then the shrimp paste and peanuts. Give it 20 minutes and don't stop until it's smooth.
2. Spoon the thick cream from the top of the coconut milk into a wok and heat until it splits and the oil rises. This is called cracking the cream.
3. Fry the paste in that oil for 5 minutes until deeply fragrant and darkened.
4. Add the meat and stir to coat. Cook 3 minutes.
5. Add the rest of the coconut milk a little at a time, letting it reduce between additions. You want a thick, glossy sauce, not a soup.
6. Season with fish sauce and palm sugar. It should be salty-sweet with a slow-building heat.
7. Stir in the shredded lime leaves. Serve topped with whole lime leaves, sliced chilli, basil, and a spoonful of coconut cream.

**Cook's note:** Store-bought paste works if you fry it properly and add a tablespoon of ground peanuts, a little extra lemongrass, and lime zest. Frying is what wakes it up.

## 2. Tom Kha Gai

Chicken in coconut broth with galangal. Not a chilli-forward soup. It should be sour, creamy, and aromatic, with the heat sitting in the background.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g chicken thigh, sliced
- 400 ml coconut milk
- 400 ml chicken stock
- 8 slices galangal
- 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and cut in lengths
- 6 kaffir lime leaves, torn
- 200 g straw or oyster mushrooms
- 4 shallots, halved
- 5 bird's eye chillies, bruised
- 4 tbsp fish sauce
- 3 tbsp lime juice, added at the end
- 1 tbsp palm sugar
- Coriander leaves

**Method**

1. Simmer the stock with the galangal, lemongrass, lime leaves, shallots, and chillies for 10 minutes so the broth takes on their flavour.
2. Add the coconut milk and bring back to a gentle simmer. Keep it below a rolling boil from now on.
3. Add the chicken and poach 6 minutes until just cooked.
4. Add the mushrooms and cook 3 minutes.
5. Season with fish sauce and palm sugar.
6. Take the pot off the heat, then stir in the lime juice. Adding it while boiling dulls the flavour and can turn the coconut grainy.
7. Taste and adjust. Sour first, then salt. Scatter coriander and serve.

**Cook's note:** The aromatics stay in the bowl and aren't meant to be eaten. Everyone at a Thai table knows to push them aside.

## 3. Phat Kaphrao

Holy basil stir fry with a fried egg on top. The national weeknight dinner. Holy basil, not Thai sweet basil, and if you can't find it, the dish is still good but it's something else.

**Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 8 min**

**Ingredients**
- 300 g coarsely chopped pork or chicken (hand-chopped beats minced)
- 5 garlic cloves
- 8 bird's eye chillies
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tsp dark soy sauce
- 1 tsp sugar
- 3 tbsp stock or water
- 2 large handfuls holy basil leaves
- 2 eggs, oil for frying
- Steamed jasmine rice

**Method**

1. Pound the garlic and chillies together into a rough paste. Don't make it smooth.
2. Heat the wok until it smokes, add oil, then the garlic and chilli. Ten seconds only.
3. Add the meat and press it flat against the wok to sear before breaking it up. Cook 3 minutes.
4. Add oyster sauce, fish sauce, dark soy, and sugar. Splash in the stock and let it bubble down into a light sauce.
5. Kill the heat, throw in the basil, and fold until it just wilts. Any longer and it goes bitter.
6. In a separate pan, fry the eggs in a shallow pool of hot oil until the whites are blistered and lacy and the yolk is still runny.
7. Serve over rice with the egg on top and prik nam pla (fish sauce with sliced chilli and lime) on the side.

**Cook's note:** Real heat matters more than technique here. If your burner is weak, cook in two smaller batches so the wok doesn't drop temperature and start steaming the meat.

## 4. Khao Niao Mamuang

Mango sticky rice. Three components and each one is simple, so any failure is usually the rice. Soak it properly and steam it, never boil.

**Serves 4 | Prep 4 hr soaking | Cook 30 min**

**Ingredients**
- 300 g glutinous rice
- 300 ml coconut milk
- 80 g sugar
- ¾ tsp salt
- 2 ripe mangoes, Nam Dok Mai or Ataulfo
- 2 tbsp toasted mung beans or sesame seeds

*Topping sauce*
- 100 ml coconut milk
- ¼ tsp salt
- 1 tsp rice flour

**Method**

1. Soak the rice in plenty of cold water for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. Skipping this gives you hard centres.
2. Drain and steam over boiling water, lined with cloth, for 20 to 25 minutes until translucent and tender. Turn it halfway through.
3. Warm the 300 ml coconut milk with the sugar and salt until dissolved. Don't boil it.
4. Tip the hot rice into a bowl and pour the sweet coconut milk over. Stir once, then cover and leave 20 minutes.
5. The rice absorbs all the liquid in that time and turns glossy. Don't panic at how wet it looks initially.
6. For the topping, whisk the remaining coconut milk with salt and rice flour and heat until slightly thickened.
7. Slice the mango, plate it beside the rice, spoon the salty coconut sauce over, and scatter the toasted seeds.

**Cook's note:** The topping sauce should taste distinctly salty on its own. That contrast against the sweet rice is the point of the dish.

## 5. Kai Yang

Grilled marinated chicken from the northeast, served with sticky rice and a tamarind or chilli dipping sauce. Coriander root is the signature flavour and worth hunting down.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min plus overnight | Cook 40 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 chicken, butterflied, or 8 thighs
- 6 coriander roots, or 8 stems with leaves stripped
- 8 garlic cloves
- 1 tbsp white peppercorns
- 3 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 2 tbsp palm sugar
- 1 tbsp turmeric powder
- 100 ml coconut milk
- 2 tbsp soy sauce

*Nam jim jaew*
- 3 tbsp tamarind paste, 2 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tbsp palm sugar, 2 tsp toasted rice powder, 1 tbsp chilli flakes, chopped shallot and coriander

**Method**

1. Pound the coriander root, garlic, and peppercorns into a paste.
2. Mix with the fish sauce, oyster sauce, palm sugar, turmeric, coconut milk, and soy.
3. Rub it into the chicken, getting under the skin, and marinate overnight.
4. Set up your grill for indirect heat, coals banked to one side.
5. Cook the chicken skin side up on the cool side, lid down, for 30 minutes.
6. Move it over the coals for the last 6 to 8 minutes, turning often, to blister the skin. Baste with leftover marinade in the first few minutes only.
7. Whisk the dipping sauce ingredients together. Rest the chicken 10 minutes, chop through the bone into pieces, and serve with sticky rice and the sauce.

**Cook's note:** Toasted rice powder is easy: dry-fry raw glutinous rice with a lime leaf until dark gold, then grind. It gives the sauce its nutty texture.

## 6. Pad Thai

Sweet, sour, and salty noodles with tamarind. The version served to tourists is usually sweeter and redder than it should be. Real pad Thai is brown, not orange.

**Serves 2 | Prep 20 min | Cook 10 min**

**Ingredients**
- 180 g dried flat rice noodles, soaked in warm water 30 minutes
- 150 g prawns, or firm tofu, or both
- 2 eggs
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 3 tbsp pressed tofu, diced
- 2 tbsp dried shrimp, chopped
- 2 tbsp sweet preserved radish, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, chopped
- 150 g bean sprouts
- 3 spring onions, in 4 cm lengths
- 60 g roasted peanuts, crushed
- Lime wedges, chilli flakes

*Sauce*
- 4 tbsp tamarind paste
- 3 tbsp palm sugar
- 3 tbsp fish sauce

**Method**

1. Dissolve the sauce ingredients together over low heat. Taste it: sour first, then sweet, then salty. Adjust now, because you won't have time later.
2. Drain the noodles. They should be pliable but still firm.
3. Heat the wok very hot. Fry the pressed tofu, dried shrimp, and radish for a minute, then the garlic for 10 seconds.
4. Add the prawns and cook until just pink. Push everything to one side.
5. Crack the eggs into the empty space, let them set for 20 seconds, then break them up roughly.
6. Add the noodles and the sauce. Toss constantly for 2 to 3 minutes until the noodles absorb the sauce and turn glossy brown. Add a splash of water if they're still stiff.
7. Throw in the sprouts and spring onion, toss twice, and serve immediately with peanuts, lime, and chilli flakes.

**Cook's note:** Cook two portions maximum at a time. Four portions in one wok drops the heat and you'll get a sticky clump instead of separate noodles.

## 7. Khao Phat

Thai fried rice. Cleaner and less sweet than Chinese versions, finished with lime and cucumber. Simple enough that the rice quality shows.

**Serves 2 | Prep 10 min | Cook 8 min**

**Ingredients**
- 3 cups cold day-old jasmine rice
- 200 g chicken, prawn, or crab meat
- 2 eggs
- 3 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 small onion, sliced
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1½ tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tsp sugar
- ½ tsp white pepper
- 2 spring onions, 1 tomato in wedges
- Cucumber slices, lime wedges, coriander
- Prik nam pla on the side

**Method**

1. Break up the cold rice with your hands so there are no clumps left.
2. Heat the wok until smoking. Add oil and the garlic, 10 seconds.
3. Add the protein and onion, stir-fry 2 minutes.
4. Push aside, scramble the eggs in the space until just set.
5. Add the rice and toss hard, using the spatula to press and separate rather than stir gently.
6. Season with fish sauce, soy, sugar, and pepper. Keep tossing 2 minutes so the rice picks up wok heat.
7. Add tomato and spring onion at the very end. Plate with cucumber, lime, and coriander, and squeeze the lime over just before eating.

**Cook's note:** The lime isn't decoration. Squeezed over at the table it lifts the whole plate, and the dish tastes flat without it.

## 8. Pla Thot

Whole fried fish, crisp all the way through, served with a sweet chilli or three-flavour sauce. Scoring and drying are what get you the crunch.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole sea bass, snapper, or tilapia, 800 g, cleaned and scaled
- 2 tsp salt
- 3 tbsp rice flour
- Oil for deep frying

*Three-flavour sauce*
- 6 garlic cloves, 6 red chillies, 3 coriander roots, pounded
- 3 tbsp tamarind paste
- 3 tbsp palm sugar
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- 100 ml water
- 2 tbsp oil

**Method**

1. Score the fish diagonally to the bone, three or four cuts each side.
2. Rub with salt inside and out and leave 15 minutes, then pat completely dry with paper towels. Any water left on the skin will spit and stop it crisping.
3. Dust lightly with rice flour, including inside the cuts.
4. Heat oil in a wide deep pan to 180°C. There should be enough to come halfway up the fish.
5. Fry 7 minutes, spooning oil over the exposed side, then turn carefully and fry 6 minutes more. Drain on a rack.
6. For the sauce, fry the pounded paste in oil for 2 minutes, then add tamarind, sugar, fish sauce, and water. Simmer until syrupy.
7. Pour the sauce over the fish and scatter with fried garlic, coriander, and sliced chilli.

**Cook's note:** A fish spatula and a second person make turning it much less nerve-wracking. If it sticks, it isn't ready to turn.

## 9. Khao Op Sapparot

Pineapple fried rice baked in the hollowed-out pineapple. Slightly theatrical, and the baking step genuinely changes the flavour rather than being decoration.

**Serves 4 | Prep 30 min | Cook 35 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 large pineapple
- 3 cups cold cooked jasmine rice
- 200 g prawns
- 100 g chicken, diced
- 2 eggs
- 3 tbsp oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 3 garlic cloves
- 50 g raisins
- 60 g cashews, toasted
- 1 tsp curry powder
- ½ tsp turmeric
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sugar
- 2 spring onions, coriander

**Method**

1. Cut the pineapple lengthwise, leaving the leaves on one half. Hollow out the flesh with a knife and spoon, leaving a 1 cm shell.
2. Dice 250 g of the flesh and set aside. Save the rest for something else.
3. Heat oil, fry the garlic and onion 2 minutes, then the chicken and prawns until just cooked.
4. Push aside, scramble the eggs, then add the rice and break it up.
5. Season with curry powder, turmeric, fish sauce, soy, and sugar. Toss over high heat 3 minutes.
6. Fold in the diced pineapple, raisins, and half the cashews. Cook 1 minute more so the pineapple doesn't turn to mush.
7. Pack into the pineapple shell, cover loosely with foil, and bake at 180°C for 20 minutes. Finish with the rest of the cashews, spring onion, and coriander.

**Cook's note:** Don't put the pineapple straight on a metal oven rack. Sit it in a baking dish, since the juices will run out of the bottom.

## 10. Tod Man Pla

Thai fish cakes, bouncy rather than soft, studded with snake beans and lime leaf. That springy texture comes from working the paste hard.

**Makes 16 | Prep 25 min | Cook 15 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g white fish fillet, cold, roughly chopped
- 3 tbsp red curry paste
- 1 egg
- 1½ tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tsp sugar
- 2 tbsp rice flour
- 80 g snake beans or green beans, sliced very thin
- 6 kaffir lime leaves, shredded fine
- Oil for frying

*Cucumber relish*
- 1 cucumber, 3 shallots, 2 red chillies, all sliced
- 80 ml rice vinegar, 60 g sugar, ½ tsp salt, 2 tbsp crushed peanuts

**Method**

1. Blend the fish with the curry paste, egg, fish sauce, sugar, and flour until it forms a sticky paste.
2. Take it out and slap it against the side of the bowl repeatedly for 3 to 5 minutes. This is what makes it bouncy instead of crumbly.
3. Fold in the beans and lime leaves by hand.
4. Chill 20 minutes, then shape into flat discs about 1.5 cm thick with wet hands.
5. Boil the vinegar, sugar, and salt for the relish, cool, then pour over the cucumber, shallot, and chilli. Add peanuts just before serving.
6. Heat 2 cm of oil to 170°C. Fry the cakes in batches for 2 to 3 minutes a side until golden and firm.
7. Drain on a rack and serve hot with the relish.

**Cook's note:** Fry one test cake first. If it falls apart, add another tablespoon of rice flour. If it's dense, you overworked it slightly, which is a much smaller problem.

## 11. Khanom Chan

Nine steamed layers of coconut and pandan pudding, peeled apart one layer at a time. It takes patience and almost no skill.

**Makes about 20 pieces | Prep 20 min | Cook 1 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 200 g tapioca starch
- 60 g rice flour
- 40 g arrowroot starch
- 250 g sugar
- 600 ml coconut milk
- 150 ml water
- ¼ tsp salt
- 60 ml pandan extract (blend 10 pandan leaves with water and strain)

**Method**

1. Dissolve the sugar in the coconut milk, water, and salt over low heat. Cool completely.
2. Whisk in the three starches until completely smooth, then strain through a fine sieve.
3. Divide the batter in half. Colour one half with the pandan extract, adjusting the other half with the same volume of water so the two batters are equally thick.
4. Steam an oiled 20 cm square tin, empty, for 5 minutes so it's hot.
5. Pour in a thin layer of green batter, about 4 mm. Steam 5 minutes with a cloth under the lid to catch drips.
6. Pour a layer of white batter over the set green. Steam 5 minutes. Repeat, alternating, for nine layers, finishing with green.
7. Steam the final layer 10 minutes. Cool completely, at least 3 hours, before cutting with an oiled knife.

**Cook's note:** Each layer must be fully set before the next goes on, or they merge. If in doubt, give it an extra minute.

## 12. Kuai-Tiao Ruea

Boat noodles. The broth is dark, heavily spiced, and traditionally thickened with pig's blood, which gives it body and a mineral depth. It's served in small bowls because it's intense.

**Serves 4, small bowls | Prep 25 min | Cook 2 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 800 g beef shin and 300 g beef bones
- 2 litres water
- 5 star anise, 2 cinnamon sticks, 1 tsp coriander seed, 4 cloves, tied in cloth
- 6 garlic cloves, 4 coriander roots, crushed
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 3 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tbsp palm sugar
- 2 tbsp pickled garlic liquid
- 150 ml pig's blood, optional
- 300 g rice noodles
- 200 g beef balls
- Morning glory, bean sprouts, coriander, fried pork rind, chilli vinegar

**Method**

1. Blanch the bones, drain, and rinse. Simmer with the water, spice bag, garlic, and coriander root for 90 minutes.
2. Add the beef shin and cook another 45 minutes until tender. Slice it.
3. Season the broth with both soy sauces, fish sauce, palm sugar, and pickled garlic liquid. It should taste strong, since the noodles will dilute it.
4. If using blood, whisk it with a ladle of hot broth first, then stir it back into the pot off the boil. Never let it boil after this or it curdles.
5. Blanch the noodles and morning glory for 20 seconds each.
6. Build small bowls: noodles, greens, sliced beef, beef balls.
7. Ladle the hot broth over and top with coriander, pork rind, and chilli vinegar. Two bowls per person is normal.

**Cook's note:** Without the blood it's still a good spiced beef noodle soup, just thinner. Add a spoonful of ground toasted rice for a little body instead.

## 13. Khao Kha Mu

Pork leg braised in soy and five spice until it collapses, served over rice with pickled greens, boiled egg, and a sharp chilli-garlic sauce.

**Serves 6 | Prep 20 min | Cook 3 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole pork hock or leg, about 2 kg, skin on
- 2 litres water
- 200 ml light soy sauce
- 4 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 100 g palm sugar
- 4 star anise, 2 cinnamon sticks, 1 tbsp five spice
- 10 garlic cloves, 5 cm ginger
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 6 eggs
- Pickled mustard greens, coriander, steamed rice

*Sauce*
- 8 bird's eye chillies, 6 garlic cloves, 3 tbsp vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp salt, blended

**Method**

1. Scrape and shave the pork skin clean. Blanch the whole hock in boiling water for 10 minutes, then rinse.
2. Caramelise the palm sugar in a large pot until it turns amber and smells slightly bitter.
3. Add the water carefully, then both soy sauces, spices, garlic, ginger, and oyster sauce.
4. Lower the pork in and simmer, covered, for 2½ to 3 hours. Turn it every 40 minutes so the colour is even.
5. Add the peeled boiled eggs for the last 30 minutes.
6. The pork is ready when the skin is gelatinous and a chopstick goes through the meat without effort.
7. Blend the chilli sauce. Chop the pork, pile it over rice, ladle braising liquid over, and add an egg, pickled greens, and plenty of the sharp sauce.

**Cook's note:** Chill the braising liquid overnight and lift off the solid fat. You'll want to keep the liquid, since it improves every time you reuse it.

## 14. Khanom Fak Bua

Small fried rice-flour cakes that puff up with a raised rim, named for their resemblance to a lotus seed pod. Crisp at the edge, chewy in the middle, and quite obscure even in Thailand.

**Makes 20 | Prep 15 min plus 1 hr rest | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 200 g rice flour
- 40 g tapioca starch
- 120 g palm sugar
- 200 ml coconut milk
- 60 ml water
- ¼ tsp salt
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
- Oil for shallow frying

**Method**

1. Melt the palm sugar in the water over low heat, then cool.
2. Whisk the rice flour, tapioca starch, and salt with the coconut milk and sugar syrup into a smooth thick batter.
3. Rest at room temperature for an hour. It should end up like thick pancake batter, coating a spoon heavily.
4. Stir in the baking powder and sesame seeds just before frying.
5. Heat 2 cm of oil to about 165°C in a small deep pan.
6. Drop in a heaped tablespoon of batter. It sinks, then rises with a puffed ring around the edge. Spoon hot oil over the top as it cooks.
7. Fry about 3 minutes total until golden and the rim is set. Drain on a rack and eat warm.

**Cook's note:** Oil temperature decides the shape. Too cool and it spreads flat, too hot and it browns before the rim can lift.

## 15. Som Tam Khai Khem

Green papaya salad with salted duck egg. The salted egg makes it richer and less brutal than the standard version, and the yolk breaks down into the dressing.

**Serves 2 | Prep 20 min | Cook 10 min**

**Ingredients**
- 300 g green papaya, peeled and shredded into long strands
- 2 salted duck eggs, boiled 10 minutes, peeled and halved
- 4 garlic cloves
- 6 bird's eye chillies, or fewer
- 10 snake beans, cut in 3 cm lengths
- 8 cherry tomatoes, halved
- 2 tbsp dried shrimp
- 3 tbsp fish sauce
- 3 tbsp lime juice
- 2 tbsp palm sugar
- 1 tbsp tamarind paste
- 3 tbsp roasted peanuts

**Method**

1. Soak the shredded papaya in ice water for 15 minutes, then drain hard. It goes crisp and loses any raw taste.
2. Pound the garlic and chillies in a clay or wooden mortar until broken but not pasty.
3. Add the palm sugar, fish sauce, lime juice, and tamarind. Pound and stir until the sugar dissolves.
4. Add the snake beans and bruise them lightly, then the dried shrimp and peanuts, giving each a few knocks.
5. Add the tomatoes and press just until they release juice.
6. Add the papaya. Now switch technique: pound with one hand while turning and lifting with a spoon in the other, so the strands bruise and soak up dressing without turning to pulp.
7. Taste and adjust, then plate with the salted egg halves on top. Break the yolks into the salad as you eat.

**Cook's note:** A granite mortar is too heavy-handed for this. Clay with a wooden pestle is the right tool, because som tam wants bruising rather than crushing.
