# African Food

Fifteen dishes from Ethiopia, Senegal, Nigeria, Kenya, Tanzania, Congo, North Africa, and Mozambique. Grouping them under one heading is a simplification, and the pantries barely overlap. Ethiopian cooking runs on berbere and spiced butter. Nigerian cooking runs on scotch bonnets, palm oil, and stock cubes. Senegalese cooking runs on onions, lemon, and mustard. East African coastal cooking runs on coconut and Indian Ocean spice.

Two of these recipes are versions of the same Senegalese dish, yassa, because you asked for both. I've written the fish and chicken versions separately, since the technique genuinely differs, and I'll say plainly that the onion base is shared.

A note on heat: scotch bonnets and habaneros are hot but also fruity, and the flavour is the reason they're used. If you want less heat, remove the seeds and white pith rather than reducing the number of peppers.

## 1. Ethiopian Beef Tibs

Cubed beef seared hard with onion, chilli, and spiced butter, served on injera. Fast, loud, and usually cooked over very high heat in a clay dish that arrives still spitting.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 700 g beef sirloin or tenderloin, in 2.5 cm cubes
- 1 large red onion, cut in thick wedges
- 3 tbsp niter kibbeh (spiced clarified butter)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 garlic cloves, chopped
- 2 cm ginger, chopped
- 2 jalapeños or 4 green chillies, sliced
- 1 tomato, in wedges
- 1 tbsp berbere
- 1 tsp mitmita, optional
- 1 tsp ground cardamom
- 2 tbsp red wine or ta'ej
- Rosemary sprig
- Salt, pepper
- Injera, and awaze sauce to serve

*Niter kibbeh, if making your own*
- 500 g butter simmered 30 min with garlic, ginger, cardamom, fenugreek, korerima, cinnamon, and nigella, then strained

**Method**

1. Dry the beef thoroughly and season with salt. Wet meat steams instead of searing.
2. Get a heavy pan or cast iron dish very hot. Add the oil, then the beef in a single layer with space between the pieces.
3. Sear 90 seconds without touching it, then turn and sear another 60 seconds. Work in two batches. Remove.
4. Add the niter kibbeh, then the onion wedges, and cook 3 minutes so they char at the edges but keep their crunch.
5. Add the garlic, ginger, and chillies for 1 minute, then the berbere, mitmita, and cardamom for 30 seconds.
6. Splash in the wine, scraping the base, then return the beef with the tomato and rosemary.
7. Toss 60 seconds, taste for salt, and serve straight from the pan onto injera with awaze on the side.

**Cook's note:** Berbere burns quickly because of the sugar in the paprika. Add it near the end and keep everything moving.

## 2. Yassa Fish

The Senegalese onion, lemon, and mustard base, built around whole grilled fish rather than chicken. The fish gets marinated, grilled, then finished in the onions so it doesn't fall apart.

**Serves 4 | Prep 25 min plus 3 hr marinating | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 4 whole fish, sea bream, tilapia, or mullet, 400 g each, scored
- 6 large onions, sliced thin
- 150 ml lemon juice, about 6 lemons
- 3 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 6 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 scotch bonnet, whole
- 3 bay leaves
- 4 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tbsp white vinegar
- 300 ml fish stock or water
- 2 tbsp green olives and 1 tbsp capers, optional
- Salt, black pepper
- Rice or attiéké to serve

**Method**

1. Combine half the lemon juice, half the mustard, the garlic, salt, and pepper. Rub it into the fish, inside and into the score marks. Refrigerate 3 hours.
2. Toss the sliced onions with the remaining lemon juice, mustard, vinegar, and a good amount of salt. Let them sit while the fish marinates. They soften and lose their harshness.
3. Wipe the marinade off the fish and grill or pan-fry 4 minutes a side until browned. Set aside carefully.
4. Heat the oil in a wide pan and add the onions with all their liquid. Cook over medium heat for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until soft, jammy, and pale gold.
5. Add the bay leaves, whole scotch bonnet, and stock. Simmer 10 minutes.
6. Lower the fish in, spoon onions over the top, cover, and simmer 8 minutes.
7. Add olives and capers, check the salt, and remove the scotch bonnet before serving over rice.

**Cook's note:** Leave the scotch bonnet whole and unpierced. It perfumes the sauce without making it inedibly hot, and you can always burst it at the end if you want more.

## 3. Chicken Suya

Nigerian grilled skewers coated in yaji, a spice mix built on ground peanuts. Sold from roadside grills at night, wrapped in newspaper with raw onion and tomato.

**Serves 4 | Prep 25 min plus 2 hr | Cook 15 min**

**Ingredients**
- 800 g chicken breast or thigh, sliced into long thin strips
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- Skewers, soaked

*Yaji (suya spice)*
- 150 g roasted peanuts, or 120 g kuli kuli, ground to powder
- 2 tbsp cayenne or ground dried chilli
- 1 tbsp smoked paprika
- 2 tsp ground ginger
- 2 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 1 tsp ground cloves, optional
- 2 tsp bouillon powder
- 1½ tsp salt

*To serve*
- Sliced raw red onion, tomato, cucumber, extra yaji, lime

**Method**

1. Grind the peanuts in short pulses until powdery. Stop before they turn into peanut butter, because oily yaji clumps and won't coat.
2. Blot the ground peanuts between paper towels to draw out some oil, then mix with the rest of the spices.
3. Toss the chicken with the oil and half the yaji. Marinate 2 hours in the fridge.
4. Thread the strips lengthwise onto skewers, keeping them flat.
5. Grill over hot coals or under a very hot broiler for 3 to 4 minutes a side. They cook fast, since the strips are thin.
6. Sprinkle the remaining dry yaji over the hot skewers as they come off. This second coating is where most of the flavour lives.
7. Serve with raw onion and tomato, extra yaji on the side, and lime.

**Cook's note:** Kuli kuli, the fried peanut snack, is the traditional base and it's already defatted. If you can find it, use it and skip the blotting.

## 4. Nkwobi

Cow foot in a thick palm oil sauce, emulsified with potash and flavoured with ehu seed. An Igbo delicacy, eaten with a cold beer and your hands.

**Serves 4 | Prep 30 min | Cook 2½ hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg cow foot, cut into pieces by the butcher
- 2 onions, one chopped, one sliced for garnish
- 2 stock cubes
- 2 tsp salt
- 200 ml palm oil
- 1 tsp powdered potash (akanwu), dissolved in 100 ml water and settled
- 1 tbsp ground ehu (calabash nutmeg) seeds, about 8 seeds toasted and ground
- 2 tbsp ground dried pepper or 3 scotch bonnets, blended
- 1 tbsp ground crayfish, optional
- Utazi leaves, sliced very thin
- Sliced onion rings, to serve

**Method**

1. Wash the cow foot thoroughly, scraping the skin.
2. Boil with the chopped onion, stock cubes, and salt for 2 to 2½ hours until the skin is soft and gelatinous and the meat pulls from the bone. Top up water as needed.
3. Drain, reserving 150 ml of the cooking liquid.
4. Pour the palm oil into a clean pot. Carefully add the clear settled potash water, a spoonful at a time, stirring hard with a wooden spoon.
5. The oil will thicken and turn from red to a pale yellow-orange emulsion. Keep stirring until it looks like a thick sauce. Stop adding potash once it's there, because too much makes it soapy.
6. Stir in the ground ehu, pepper, crayfish, and a splash of the reserved stock over low heat.
7. Fold the cow foot through until every piece is coated. Serve in a wooden mortar, topped with utazi and onion rings.

**Cook's note:** Potash is strong stuff. Use the clear liquid only, never the sediment, and add it gradually. Bicarbonate of soda will emulsify the oil too, though it changes the flavour.

## 5. Chicken Brochettes

Grilled marinated skewers, a Central and East African staple sold everywhere from Kinshasa to Bujumbura. Usually served with fried plantain and a chilli sauce called pili pili.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min plus 4 hr | Cook 15 min**

**Ingredients**
- 800 g chicken thigh, in 3 cm cubes
- 1 onion, cut into squares
- 1 red pepper, cut into squares
- Skewers, soaked

*Marinade*
- 4 tbsp neutral oil
- Juice of 2 lemons
- 5 garlic cloves, crushed
- 3 cm ginger, grated
- 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp curry powder
- 1 tsp thyme
- 1 stock cube, crumbled
- Salt, pepper

*Pili pili sauce*
- 6 scotch bonnets, 4 garlic cloves, 1 small onion, 2 tbsp oil, juice of 1 lemon, salt, blended and briefly fried

**Method**

1. Whisk the marinade and pour it over the chicken. Refrigerate at least 4 hours, overnight if you can.
2. Thread the chicken onto skewers, alternating with onion and pepper squares. Leave a little space between pieces so heat gets in.
3. Blend the pili pili ingredients, then fry the mixture in a small pan for 5 minutes to take the raw edge off. Cool.
4. Build a hot charcoal fire and let the flames settle to glowing coals.
5. Grill 10 to 12 minutes, turning every 3 minutes, basting with leftover marinade only in the first half of cooking.
6. Move to a cooler part of the grill if the outsides char before the centre is done.
7. Rest 5 minutes and serve with fried plantain, rice, and plenty of pili pili.

**Cook's note:** Stop basting in the last five minutes. Raw marinade needs heat and time to become safe, and brushing it on at the end doesn't give it either.

## 6. Kuku Paka

Swahili coast chicken in coconut curry, grilled first for char then simmered in a coconut and lime sauce. The blend of East African and Indian cooking that defines the Kenyan and Tanzanian coast.

**Serves 4 | Prep 25 min | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 chicken, jointed into 8, or 8 thighs, skin on
- 2 tbsp oil

*Marinade*
- 4 garlic cloves, 3 cm ginger, blended to a paste
- Juice of 1 lime
- 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp salt

*Sauce*
- 2 onions, chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, 3 cm ginger
- 2 tomatoes, chopped
- 2 green chillies
- 1 tbsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp cumin
- 1 tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 400 ml coconut milk
- Juice of 1 lime
- Large handful coriander leaves
- Salt
- 4 boiled eggs, halved
- 3 potatoes, boiled and halved

**Method**

1. Slash the chicken pieces, rub with the marinade, and rest 30 minutes.
2. Grill or broil the chicken until charred in patches and about three quarters cooked, roughly 15 minutes. The char is essential to the dish.
3. Meanwhile fry the onions in oil for 10 minutes until golden. Add garlic, ginger, and chillies for 2 minutes.
4. Add the dry spices, cook 1 minute, then the tomatoes and cook until they break down.
5. Blend half this base with a little coconut milk for a smoother sauce, then return it to the pan with the rest of the coconut milk.
6. Add the chicken and any juices from the grill tray. Simmer gently for 15 minutes.
7. Add the potatoes and eggs to warm through, then finish with lime juice and a lot of chopped coriander. Serve with rice or chapati.

**Cook's note:** Don't skip the grilling step by simply frying the chicken. The smoky bitterness against the sweet coconut is the entire character of the dish.

## 7. Shakshuka

Eggs poached in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce. North African in origin, adopted across the Middle East. Simple enough that the sauce needs to be genuinely good, not just quick.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 35 min**

**Ingredients**
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, sliced
- 2 red peppers, sliced
- 4 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 800 g tinned plum tomatoes, crushed by hand
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 2 tsp sweet paprika
- ½ tsp caraway seeds, crushed
- 1 tsp harissa, or 1 chopped chilli
- 1 tsp sugar
- Salt, black pepper
- 6 eggs
- 100 g feta, crumbled
- Parsley, coriander, and good bread

**Method**

1. Cook the onion and peppers in the oil over medium-low heat for 15 minutes until genuinely soft and sweet. Rushing this leaves the sauce sharp.
2. Add the garlic and cook 2 minutes.
3. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 2 minutes until it darkens.
4. Add the spices and harissa, cook 1 minute, then the tomatoes and sugar.
5. Simmer 15 to 20 minutes until thick enough that a spoon dragged through leaves a trail that holds for a second. Season well.
6. Make wells with the back of a spoon and crack an egg into each. Season the eggs themselves.
7. Cover and cook 6 to 8 minutes until the whites are set and the yolks are still runny. Scatter feta and herbs and bring the pan to the table with bread.

**Cook's note:** Take it off the heat when the whites look almost but not quite set. Carryover cooking finishes them, and overcooked yolks defeat the purpose.

## 8. Harissa Chicken Thighs

Roasted thighs under a harissa, honey, and lemon glaze. Weeknight food, one tray, and the skin gets properly crisp if you give it space.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min plus 1 hr | Cook 45 min**

**Ingredients**
- 8 chicken thighs, bone in, skin on
- 3 tbsp harissa paste
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp honey
- Juice and zest of 1 lemon
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- ½ tsp cinnamon
- 1½ tsp salt

*Tray vegetables*
- 500 g new potatoes, halved
- 2 red onions, in wedges
- 1 lemon, sliced
- 200 g cherry tomatoes
- Coriander, and yoghurt with lemon to serve

**Method**

1. Whisk the harissa, oil, honey, lemon, garlic, spices, and salt.
2. Reserve 2 tbsp for the end, then coat the chicken with the rest and marinate at least an hour.
3. Toss the potatoes and onions with a little oil and salt and spread on a large tray.
4. Roast at 200°C for 20 minutes on their own.
5. Nestle the chicken on top, skin side up, leaving space between pieces so they roast rather than steam. Tuck in the lemon slices.
6. Roast 25 minutes, adding the tomatoes for the last 10.
7. Brush with the reserved harissa mixture and grill 3 minutes to glaze. Rest 5 minutes, scatter coriander, and serve with the lemon yoghurt.

**Cook's note:** Honey burns. Save the glaze for the last three minutes under the grill rather than putting it all on at the start.

## 9. Sukuma Wiki

Braised collard greens with onion and tomato, eaten daily across Kenya with ugali. The name translates roughly as "stretch the week," which tells you what it's for.

**Serves 4 | Prep 10 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 600 g collard greens or kale, stems removed, rolled and sliced thin
- 3 tbsp oil
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 3 tomatoes, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 green chilli, chopped, optional
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1 stock cube or 1 tsp salt
- 150 ml water or stock
- Juice of ½ lemon

**Method**

1. Stack the leaves, roll them into a tight cigar, and slice across into thin ribbons. Uniform cutting matters more here than you'd think, since it's what makes them cook evenly.
2. Heat the oil and cook the onion for 6 minutes until soft and starting to colour.
3. Add the garlic and chilli for 1 minute.
4. Add the tomatoes and coriander and cook 5 minutes until they collapse into a rough sauce.
5. Add the greens in handfuls, stirring so each batch wilts before you add the next.
6. Pour in the water, add the stock cube, cover, and simmer 8 to 10 minutes until tender but still green.
7. Uncover, cook off any excess liquid, and finish with lemon juice. Serve with ugali and grilled meat.

**Cook's note:** Kale cooks faster than collards. If you're using kale, cut the covered simmer to five minutes or you'll end up with something grey and sad.

## 10. Pan-Seared Tilapia

A simple fillet with a spice crust, quick pan sauce, and lemon. Fifteen minutes start to finish, and the technique transfers to any firm white fish.

**Serves 4 | Prep 10 min | Cook 12 min**

**Ingredients**
- 4 tilapia fillets, about 180 g each
- 2 tbsp plain flour or fine cornmeal
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp cayenne
- ½ tsp dried thyme
- 1 tsp salt, ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 40 g butter
- 3 garlic cloves, sliced
- Juice of 1 lemon
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley

**Method**

1. Pat the fillets completely dry on both sides. This matters more than anything else in the recipe.
2. Mix the flour with all the dry spices and dust the fish lightly, shaking off the excess.
3. Heat the oil in a non-stick or well-seasoned pan over medium-high until it shimmers.
4. Lay the fillets in presentation side down and press gently for 5 seconds so they make full contact.
5. Cook 3 to 4 minutes without moving them. When the edges turn opaque and the crust releases easily, flip once.
6. Cook 2 minutes more, then add the butter and garlic and baste the fish for 30 seconds as the butter foams.
7. Squeeze in the lemon off the heat, scatter parsley, and spoon the pan sauce over. Serve with rice or greens.

**Cook's note:** Tilapia is thin and goes from done to dry in under a minute. Pull it when the thickest part just flakes and no sooner.

## 11. Poulet Yassa

The chicken version of Senegal's yassa. Same onion, lemon, and mustard base as the fish, but the chicken marinates in it first and the onions cook down much longer.

**Serves 6 | Prep 25 min plus overnight | Cook 1 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1 chicken, jointed into 8, or 8 thighs and drumsticks
- 8 large onions, sliced thin
- 200 ml lemon juice, about 8 lemons
- 4 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 8 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 scotch bonnet, left whole
- 4 bay leaves
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 400 ml chicken stock
- 2 stock cubes
- 3 tbsp green olives
- 1 carrot, julienned, optional
- Salt, black pepper
- Broken rice to serve

**Method**

1. Combine the onions, lemon juice, mustard, garlic, salt, and pepper in a large bowl. Add the chicken and turn everything so the onions are packed around it.
2. Refrigerate overnight, or at least 6 hours. The onions release water and soften in the acid.
3. Lift the chicken out, brushing the onions back into the bowl, and pat it dry.
4. Sear the chicken in oil until browned on all sides. Remove.
5. Add all the onions and marinade to the pot and cook over medium heat for 30 minutes, stirring often, until they collapse into a soft golden mass.
6. Add the stock, stock cubes, bay leaves, and whole scotch bonnet. Return the chicken, cover, and simmer 25 minutes.
7. Add olives and carrot for the last 5 minutes. Remove the scotch bonnet, check the seasoning, and serve over broken rice with the onions heaped on top.

**Cook's note:** Eight onions is correct and it isn't a typo. They reduce to about a quarter of their volume and they are the sauce, not a background ingredient.

## 12. Peppered Gizzard

Nigerian party food: chicken gizzards boiled tender, fried, then tossed in a fried pepper sauce. Chewy in a good way, and cheap.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 1 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 700 g chicken gizzards, cleaned and trimmed of silver skin
- 1 onion, quartered
- 2 bay leaves, 1 stock cube, 1 tsp thyme, 1 tsp salt
- Oil for shallow frying

*Pepper sauce*
- 4 red bell peppers, deseeded
- 4 scotch bonnets, adjust to taste
- 2 onions, one blended and one sliced
- 4 garlic cloves, 3 cm ginger
- 4 tbsp neutral oil or palm oil
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 stock cube
- 1 tsp curry powder, 1 tsp thyme
- Salt
- Spring onion and parsley to finish

**Method**

1. Boil the gizzards with the onion, bay, thyme, stock cube, and salt for 40 to 50 minutes until tender enough to bite through cleanly. They'll never be soft, and they aren't meant to be.
2. Drain and dry them well, then cut into bite-sized pieces.
3. Shallow fry in hot oil for 4 minutes until browned and slightly crisp at the edges. Drain.
4. Blend the bell peppers, scotch bonnets, one onion, garlic, and ginger into a coarse purée.
5. Fry the sliced onion in oil for 5 minutes, add the tomato paste for 2 minutes, then pour in the blended pepper.
6. Fry the sauce over medium heat for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring, until the water cooks off and the oil rises to the surface. This is the step people skip and it's the one that matters.
7. Season with the stock cube, curry powder, and thyme, then fold in the gizzards and cook 5 minutes. Finish with spring onion and parsley.

**Cook's note:** Raw blended pepper tastes sour and thin. You're looking for the sauce to darken and for oil to separate out at the edges, which takes a solid twenty minutes.

## 13. Peri-Peri Chicken Wings

Mozambican and South African chilli sauce built on African bird's eye chillies, lemon, and garlic. The sauce goes on twice: once as a marinade, once at the end.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min plus 4 hr | Cook 40 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg chicken wings, split into flats and drums
- 1 tsp baking powder, 1 tsp salt

*Peri-peri sauce*
- 15 African bird's eye chillies, or 8 red chillies plus 3 scotch bonnets
- 1 red pepper, roasted and peeled
- 6 garlic cloves
- 1 onion, roughly chopped
- Zest and juice of 2 lemons
- 3 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 2 tsp salt
- 2 bay leaves

**Method**

1. Blend all the sauce ingredients except the bay leaves until smooth.
2. Simmer the sauce with the bay leaves for 15 minutes to cook out the raw onion and garlic. Cool, then split it in two.
3. Toss the dried wings with the baking powder and salt, then coat in half the sauce. Refrigerate 4 hours.
4. Arrange on a rack over a tray, spaced apart, and roast at 200°C for 25 minutes.
5. Turn and roast 12 minutes more until the skin is crisp and blistered.
6. Brush generously with the reserved fresh sauce.
7. Grill for 3 minutes to set the glaze, then serve with lemon wedges and more sauce for dipping.

**Cook's note:** The baking powder raises the skin's pH and helps it brown and crisp. It's a small amount and you won't taste it, but skip it and the wings come out noticeably softer.

## 14. Mafe

Senegalese groundnut stew. Peanut butter, tomato, and lamb or beef, cooked slowly until the oil separates. Rich, and one bowl goes a long way.

**Serves 6 | Prep 25 min | Cook 2 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg lamb shoulder or beef chuck, in large cubes
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 2 onions, chopped
- 5 garlic cloves, crushed
- 3 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 tomatoes, chopped
- 250 g unsweetened smooth peanut butter
- 1.2 litres beef stock or water
- 2 stock cubes
- 1 scotch bonnet, whole
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 2 carrots, 2 potatoes, 1 sweet potato, ½ small cabbage, cut into large chunks
- 100 g okra, optional
- Salt
- White rice to serve

**Method**

1. Brown the meat hard in oil, in batches. Set aside.
2. Cook the onions for 10 minutes until golden, then the garlic for 1 minute.
3. Add the tomato paste and fry 3 minutes until it turns brick red and loses its metallic edge.
4. Loosen the peanut butter with a cup of warm stock, whisking until smooth, then stir it into the pot.
5. Return the meat, add the remaining stock, stock cubes, bay, whole scotch bonnet, and pepper. Simmer uncovered for 1 hour, stirring often since peanut butter catches easily.
6. Add the root vegetables and cook 25 minutes, then the cabbage and okra for the last 10.
7. The stew is ready when a layer of peanut oil floats on the surface and the sauce coats a spoon. Remove the scotch bonnet, salt to taste, and serve over rice.

**Cook's note:** Stir the bottom of the pot every ten minutes or so. Peanut butter sinks and scorches, and burnt mafe is unrecoverable.

## 15. Fumbwa

Congolese wild spinach stewed with peanut and smoked fish. Fumbwa itself is a forest vine leaf, sold dried, and spinach is the usual stand-in outside Central Africa.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min plus soaking | Cook 50 min**

**Ingredients**
- 200 g dried fumbwa leaves, soaked overnight, or 800 g fresh spinach
- 1 smoked mackerel or 200 g smoked fish, flaked, bones removed
- 150 g unsalted peanuts, ground, or 4 tbsp peanut butter
- 4 tbsp palm oil
- 2 onions, chopped
- 4 garlic cloves
- 3 tomatoes, chopped, plus 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 scotch bonnet, chopped or whole
- 1 stock cube
- 400 ml water or stock
- Salt
- Rice, fufu, or plantain to serve

**Method**

1. If using dried fumbwa, soak overnight, then boil for 30 minutes until tender and drain. Fresh spinach needs only a quick blanch and a hard squeeze.
2. Chop the leaves finely.
3. Heat the palm oil until it just begins to shimmer, not smoke, then cook the onions for 8 minutes.
4. Add the garlic, tomato paste, and chopped tomatoes, and cook 8 minutes until thick.
5. Stir in the ground peanuts loosened with a little water, plus the stock cube and scotch bonnet. Simmer 10 minutes.
6. Add the leaves and the smoked fish. Simmer 15 minutes, stirring, so the flavours combine and the sauce thickens around the greens.
7. Taste for salt. Smoked fish and stock cubes are both salty, so season last. Serve with rice, fufu, or fried plantain.

**Cook's note:** Overheating palm oil destroys its flavour and turns it acrid. Warm it gently and stop as soon as it loosens and moves freely in the pan.
