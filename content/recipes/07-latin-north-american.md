# Latin & North American Food

Seventeen recipes spanning Mexico, Peru, Colombia, the United States, and Canada. The Peruvian recipes lean on ají amarillo and ají panca, two chilli pastes that define the cuisine and have no real substitute. Buy them frozen or in jars from a Latin grocer or online. The Mexican recipes use dried chillies, which are worth toasting yourself.

For the American section, the throughline is fat and time. Brisket takes twelve hours. Chowder is better the next day. A cheeseburger takes four minutes and is mostly about not overworking the meat.

---

## 1. Birria Tacos

Slow-braised chilli beef, shredded into tortillas dipped in the fat from the braise, griddled until crisp, and served with a cup of consommé for dunking.

**Serves 8 | Prep 40 min | Cook 4 hr**

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 kg beef chuck, plus 1 kg beef short rib or shin on the bone
- 6 guajillo chillies, 4 ancho chillies, 2 chiles de árbol, all stemmed and seeded
- 1 onion, halved
- 8 garlic cloves
- 3 tomatoes
- 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 1 tbsp Mexican oregano
- 2 tsp cumin, 1 tsp coriander
- 4 cloves, 1 cinnamon stick, 6 peppercorns, 2 bay leaves
- 2 tbsp salt
- 2 litres beef stock

*To assemble*
- 24 corn tortillas
- 400 g Oaxaca cheese or low-moisture mozzarella, shredded
- White onion, coriander, lime

**Method**

1. Toast the dried chillies in a dry pan for 30 seconds a side. They should smell fragrant and soften slightly. Any smoke means burnt, and burnt chillies taste bitter through the whole pot.
2. Cover the chillies with boiling water and soak 20 minutes.
3. Char the onion, garlic, and tomatoes in the same dry pan until blackened in patches.
4. Blend the drained chillies with the charred vegetables, vinegar, spices, salt, and a cup of the soaking water until completely smooth. Push it through a sieve.
5. Sear the beef hard, then combine with the chilli purée and stock in a heavy pot. It should be nearly covered.
6. Cover and cook at 160°C for 3½ to 4 hours until the meat shreds under a fork.
7. Skim the red fat off the top and reserve it. Shred the meat. Dip a tortilla in that fat, lay it on a hot griddle, add cheese and meat, fold, and fry 2 minutes a side until crisp. Serve with consommé, onion, coriander, and lime.

**Cook's note:** Sieving the chilli purée takes five minutes and removes all the skin fragments. It's the single biggest upgrade to the texture of the consommé.

## 2. Tres Leches Cake

A sponge built to absorb, soaked in three milks and topped with whipped cream. The cake must be dry and open-crumbed, which is why there's no butter in it.

**Serves 12 | Prep 30 min plus 6 hr chilling | Cook 30 min**

**Ingredients**

*Cake*
- 5 eggs, separated
- 200 g caster sugar
- 180 g plain flour
- 1½ tsp baking powder
- 80 ml whole milk
- 1 tsp vanilla
- ¼ tsp salt

*Soak*
- 400 g condensed milk
- 350 ml evaporated milk
- 250 ml heavy cream
- 2 tbsp rum or brandy, optional

*Topping*
- 400 ml heavy cream
- 3 tbsp icing sugar
- Cinnamon, or fresh strawberries

**Method**

1. Beat the yolks with three quarters of the sugar until pale and thick, about 5 minutes. Add the milk and vanilla.
2. Fold in the sifted flour, baking powder, and salt.
3. Whip the whites to soft peaks, add the remaining sugar, and beat to firm peaks. Fold into the batter in three additions.
4. Pour into a lined 23 by 33 cm tin and bake at 175°C for 25 to 30 minutes until springy and golden.
5. Cool completely in the tin. Poke holes all over with a skewer, going right to the base.
6. Whisk the three milks together and pour slowly over the cake, a third at a time, waiting for each to soak in. It will look like too much liquid. It isn't.
7. Cover and refrigerate at least 6 hours, ideally overnight. Whip the topping cream with icing sugar, spread thickly, and dust with cinnamon.

**Cook's note:** Soak a cooled cake, never a warm one. Warm sponge turns gummy and won't take the liquid evenly.

## 3. Lomo Saltado

Peru's Chinese-Peruvian stir fry: beef, onion, tomato, and chips, tossed in soy and vinegar, served with rice. Yes, chips and rice on the same plate. That's the dish.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 600 g beef sirloin or tenderloin, in thick strips
- 2 red onions, cut into thick wedges
- 3 tomatoes, in wedges, seeds removed
- 1 ají amarillo, sliced, plus 1 tbsp ají amarillo paste
- 4 garlic cloves, chopped
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 600 g potatoes, cut into thick chips and fried
- Coriander, salt, pepper, oil
- Steamed white rice

**Method**

1. Fry the chips until golden and crisp. Drain and keep warm. They must be ready before you start the stir fry.
2. Season the beef heavily with salt and pepper. Dry it well.
3. Get a wok or wide pan as hot as it will go. Add oil, then the beef in a single layer. Leave it 60 seconds to sear before moving it.
4. Cook 2 minutes total, then remove. It should be rare in the middle.
5. Add the onions and ají amarillo and cook 90 seconds. They should stay crunchy at the core.
6. Add the garlic for 15 seconds, then the vinegar and soy, letting it hiss and reduce. Add the ají paste and oyster sauce.
7. Return the beef with the tomatoes, toss 30 seconds, then fold in the chips and coriander at the very end. Serve immediately with rice.

**Cook's note:** Everything happens fast, so have all your ingredients cut and lined up before you turn on the heat. The onions and tomatoes should still have bite.

## 4. Chicha Morada

Purple corn drink, boiled with pineapple skin, cinnamon, and clove, then sweetened and sharpened with lime. Non-alcoholic and served with almost everything in Peru.

**Makes 2 litres | Prep 10 min | Cook 50 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g dried purple corn (maíz morado), whole cobs or kernels
- 3 litres water
- Skin and core of 1 pineapple, well washed
- 2 apples, quartered, or their peels
- 2 cinnamon sticks
- 6 cloves
- 4 allspice berries
- 150 to 200 g sugar
- Juice of 3 limes
- Finely diced apple and pineapple, to serve

**Method**

1. Rinse the corn. If using whole cobs, break them into pieces so the cob goes in too, since it carries a lot of the colour.
2. Put the corn, water, pineapple skin, apple, and whole spices in a large pot.
3. Bring to a boil, then simmer uncovered for 45 minutes. The water turns dark purple, almost black.
4. Strain through a fine sieve or cloth, pressing the solids gently. Discard everything solid.
5. Cool to room temperature, then stir in the sugar until dissolved. Taste as you go, since sweetness is personal here.
6. Add the lime juice last. It brightens the flavour and shifts the colour slightly toward red.
7. Chill thoroughly and serve over ice with the diced fruit floating in it.

**Cook's note:** Add lime only after the liquid has cooled. Hot lime juice tastes cooked and slightly bitter.

## 5. Tallarines Verdes

Peruvian green pasta, related to pesto but built on spinach and evaporated milk rather than pine nuts and oil. Creamier, milder, and usually served with a fried steak on top.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g spaghetti or fettuccine
- 250 g spinach, blanched 30 seconds and squeezed dry
- 60 g fresh basil leaves
- 1 onion, roughly chopped
- 4 garlic cloves
- 200 ml evaporated milk
- 100 g queso fresco, or feta rinsed of salt
- 60 g grated Parmesan
- 60 g walnuts or pecans
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- Salt, pepper
- 4 thin beef steaks, for serving

**Method**

1. Fry the onion and garlic in the olive oil until soft and lightly golden, about 8 minutes. Cool slightly.
2. Blend the spinach, basil, cooked onion and garlic, nuts, both cheeses, and the evaporated milk until smooth and bright green.
3. Season well. It should taste slightly too strong on its own, since the pasta will mute it.
4. Boil the pasta in well-salted water until al dente. Reserve a cup of the water.
5. Warm the sauce gently in a wide pan. Do not boil it, or the milk will split and the green will dull.
6. Drain the pasta and toss it into the sauce with a splash of pasta water until it coats evenly.
7. Season the steaks, fry them hard for 90 seconds a side, and serve on top of the pasta.

**Cook's note:** Blanching the spinach and squeezing it very dry keeps the sauce from being watery and keeps the colour vivid. Raw spinach gives a greyer result.

## 6. Pescado Frito

Whole fried fish, coastal Latin America's default plate, served with rice, patacones, and a sharp salad. Crisp skin, moist flesh, lime everywhere.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 4 whole fish, mojarra, snapper, or sea bream, 400 g each, scaled and gutted
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- Juice of 3 limes
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp paprika
- 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper
- 120 g fine cornmeal or masarepa
- 60 g plain flour
- Oil for frying

*To serve*
- Patacones (twice-fried green plantain), coconut rice, lime wedges
- Salad of tomato, red onion, coriander, lime, and salt

**Method**

1. Score each fish three times per side, cutting down to the bone.
2. Rub with garlic, lime juice, cumin, paprika, salt, and pepper, working it into the cuts. Rest 30 minutes.
3. Pat the fish dry on the outside. The marinade stays in the cuts, but the skin needs to be dry.
4. Mix the cornmeal and flour and dredge each fish, pressing it into the scores.
5. Heat 3 cm of oil to 180°C in a wide pan.
6. Fry 6 to 7 minutes on the first side without moving it, then turn once and fry 5 minutes more. The fin should pull out easily when it's done.
7. Drain on a rack, salt lightly, and serve with everything else and a lot of lime.

**Cook's note:** Fry one fish at a time unless your pan is enormous. Two fish drop the oil temperature and you get greasy skin instead of crisp.

## 7. Papa a la Huancaína

Boiled potatoes under a creamy ají amarillo sauce. A starter, and one of the easiest ways into Peruvian food since it takes fifteen minutes.

**Serves 6 | Prep 15 min | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg yellow potatoes, boiled whole in salted water, peeled and sliced thick
- 4 ají amarillo, seeded and deveined, or 4 tbsp ají amarillo paste
- 200 g queso fresco
- 150 ml evaporated milk
- 8 saltine crackers
- 1 small onion, chopped
- 2 garlic cloves
- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- Salt

*To serve*
- Boiled eggs, black olives, lettuce leaves

**Method**

1. If using fresh ají, boil the chillies three times in fresh water, 5 minutes each time, draining between. This removes most of the heat while keeping the fruity flavour.
2. Fry the onion and garlic in oil for 6 minutes, then add the ají and cook 3 minutes more.
3. Blend that mixture with the cheese, evaporated milk, and crackers until completely smooth.
4. Check the consistency. It should pour slowly and coat a spoon thickly. Add milk to loosen or another cracker to thicken.
5. Season with salt. Chill for at least 30 minutes so it firms slightly.
6. Lay lettuce on plates, arrange the potato slices over it.
7. Pour the sauce over generously and top each plate with a halved boiled egg and a few olives.

**Cook's note:** Crackers are the real thickener. They're not a shortcut, they're the traditional method, and bread doesn't behave the same way.

## 8. Crispy Colombian Empanadas

Corn dough shells with potato and beef inside, deep fried until the outside shatters. The dough is masarepa, not wheat, which is why the texture is so different from Argentine empanadas.

**Makes 20 | Prep 45 min | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**

*Dough*
- 400 g masarepa (precooked yellow cornmeal)
- 700 ml warm water
- 1 tbsp achiote or ½ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tbsp oil

*Filling*
- 400 g potatoes, boiled and roughly mashed
- 300 g minced beef, or beef and pork
- 1 onion, 3 spring onions, 2 tomatoes, all finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves
- 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp achiote, ½ tsp black pepper
- Coriander, salt
- Oil for deep frying

*Ají picante*
- Chopped spring onion, coriander, lime juice, vinegar, water, chopped chilli, salt

**Method**

1. Cook the onion, spring onion, garlic, and tomato in oil for 8 minutes until soft.
2. Add the beef and spices, brown it well, then fold in the mashed potato and coriander. Season firmly and cool completely.
3. Mix the masarepa with the water, achiote, salt, and oil. Knead 3 minutes, then rest 10 minutes. It should feel like soft playdough, smooth with no cracks when you roll a ball.
4. Cut a plastic bag open. Put a golf-ball-sized piece of dough between the sheets and press flat to 3 mm with a plate.
5. Spoon filling onto one half, then use the plastic to fold the dough over and seal, pressing the edge firmly. Trim with a bowl rim for a neat half-moon.
6. Heat oil to 180°C. Fry in batches for 4 to 5 minutes, turning, until deep golden and blistered.
7. Drain on a rack and serve hot with ají.

**Cook's note:** If the dough cracks when folded, it's too dry. Work in a tablespoon of warm water at a time until it stops.

## 9. Chicken Burrito Bowls

Not traditional anything, but a genuinely good weeknight assembly. Everything can be made ahead and the components keep separately for four days.

**Serves 4 | Prep 25 min | Cook 30 min**

**Ingredients**

*Chicken*
- 700 g chicken thighs
- 2 tbsp oil, juice of 2 limes
- 3 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp chipotle in adobo
- 2 tsp cumin, 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp oregano, 1 tsp salt

*Cilantro lime rice*
- 300 g long-grain rice, 1 bay leaf
- Juice of 1 lime, large handful chopped coriander, 1 tbsp oil, salt

*Black beans*
- 2 tins black beans, drained
- 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp cumin, 1 bay leaf, 200 ml stock

*To build*
- Pico de gallo, corn salsa, guacamole, sour cream, grated cheese, shredded lettuce, pickled red onion

**Method**

1. Blend the chicken marinade and coat the thighs. Leave 30 minutes minimum, longer if you have it.
2. Cook the rice with the bay leaf and salt. When done, fluff and fold through the lime juice, oil, and coriander while still hot.
3. Fry the onion and garlic for the beans, add cumin, beans, bay leaf, and stock, and simmer 15 minutes until thickened. Mash a spoonful against the side to give it body.
4. Sear the chicken in a very hot pan or on a grill, 5 minutes a side, until charred at the edges.
5. Rest 5 minutes, then chop into rough pieces.
6. Warm the bowls if you're being nice about it.
7. Build in order: rice, beans, chicken, then everything cold on top. Squeeze lime over at the end.

**Cook's note:** Salt each component separately as you go. A bowl seasoned only at the end tastes flat in the middle no matter how much you add on top.

## 10. Chilaquiles

Fried tortillas simmered briefly in salsa, topped with egg, cheese, and cream. Breakfast, and the classic use for stale tortillas. Timing is everything, since they go soggy fast.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 25 min**

**Ingredients**
- 16 corn tortillas, cut into 6 wedges each, ideally a day old
- Oil for frying

*Salsa roja*
- 6 tomatoes, 4 guajillo chillies, 2 chiles de árbol
- ½ onion, 3 garlic cloves
- 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp oregano
- 300 ml chicken stock, salt

*To serve*
- 4 fried eggs
- Crumbled queso fresco or feta
- Crema or sour cream
- Sliced red onion, coriander, avocado
- Shredded chicken, optional

**Method**

1. Fry the tortilla wedges in batches at 180°C until golden and rigid. Drain on paper and salt lightly. Baking at 200°C with a little oil for 15 minutes also works.
2. Toast the dried chillies briefly, then soak in hot water for 15 minutes.
3. Char the tomatoes, onion, and garlic under a hot grill until blistered and blackened in spots.
4. Blend everything with the cumin, oregano, and a little soaking water until smooth.
5. Fry the salsa in 2 tbsp of hot oil for 5 minutes so it darkens and thickens, then add the stock and simmer 10 minutes. Season.
6. Bring the salsa to a hard simmer, add the chips, and fold for 60 to 90 seconds only. They should be coated and softening at the edges but still have structure.
7. Get them onto plates immediately with egg, cheese, crema, onion, coriander, and avocado.

**Cook's note:** Serve within two minutes of adding the chips. Chilaquiles have a short window between perfect and mush, and there's no way to slow it down.

## 11. Classic Cheeseburger

Two thin patties, American cheese, soft bun. The smashing technique gives you a crust across the whole surface, which is where the flavour lives.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 10 min**

**Ingredients**
- 600 g ground beef chuck, 20% fat, loosely formed into 8 balls of 75 g
- 8 slices American cheese
- 4 soft potato or brioche buns
- 2 tbsp butter
- Salt and coarse black pepper
- Sliced dill pickles, thin white onion, shredded lettuce, tomato

*Sauce*
- 4 tbsp mayonnaise, 2 tbsp ketchup, 1 tbsp yellow mustard, 2 tbsp chopped pickle, 1 tsp pickle brine, ½ tsp paprika

**Method**

1. Handle the beef as little as possible. Roll each portion loosely into a ball and don't compact it.
2. Mix the sauce and chill it. Butter and toast the cut sides of the buns in a pan until golden.
3. Get a cast iron pan or flat griddle screaming hot. Wipe it with a little oil.
4. Place two balls on the surface, wait 10 seconds, then smash each flat with a stiff spatula, hard, to about 1 cm. Hold for 5 seconds.
5. Season the top with salt and pepper. Cook 90 seconds until the edges are dark brown and lacy.
6. Scrape underneath with a sharp-edged spatula so all the crust comes with it, flip, and add cheese immediately. Cook 45 seconds more.
7. Stack two patties per bun with sauce, pickles, onion, lettuce, and tomato. Eat right away.

**Cook's note:** Smash within the first 30 seconds or not at all. Once the surface has set, pressing just squeezes out juice and gives you a dry patty.

## 12. Crab Imperial

A Chesapeake Bay classic: lump crab meat bound in a light mayonnaise mixture and baked until golden. The whole point is minimal binder and maximum crab.

**Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 20 min**

**Ingredients**
- 500 g jumbo lump blue crab meat, picked over gently for shell
- 4 tbsp mayonnaise
- 1 egg yolk
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1½ tsp Old Bay seasoning
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp finely chopped red pepper
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley
- 20 g melted butter
- 2 tbsp panko, optional
- Paprika, lemon wedges

**Method**

1. Whisk the mayonnaise, yolk, mustard, Worcestershire, Old Bay, and lemon juice into a smooth binder.
2. Fold in the red pepper and parsley.
3. Add the crab and fold with a rubber spatula, lifting from underneath, using no more than six or seven strokes. The lumps must stay whole, because broken crab turns the dish into a paste.
4. Divide between four ramekins or scallop shells, mounding it rather than pressing it flat.
5. Drizzle with the melted butter and scatter panko over if you want a crunchy top.
6. Dust with paprika and bake at 190°C for 15 to 18 minutes until the tops are golden and the centre is hot.
7. Rest 3 minutes and serve with lemon and nothing else that competes.

**Cook's note:** Pick through the crab with your fingers rather than a fork. You'll find the shell fragments and you won't shred the meat doing it.

## 13. New England Clam Chowder

Thick, white, and made with salt pork or bacon. The clam liquor is the base, so don't throw it away, and don't add flour so heavily that it turns to wallpaper paste.

**Serves 6 | Prep 25 min | Cook 40 min**

**Ingredients**
- 2 kg fresh quahogs or littleneck clams, scrubbed, or 500 g chopped clams plus 500 ml clam juice
- 250 ml water or dry white wine
- 120 g salt pork or thick bacon, diced
- 1 large onion, diced
- 2 celery sticks, diced
- 2 tbsp plain flour
- 700 g floury potatoes, in 2 cm cubes
- 2 bay leaves, 3 sprigs thyme
- 500 ml whole milk
- 250 ml heavy cream
- 30 g butter
- Salt, white pepper
- Oyster crackers, chives

**Method**

1. Steam the clams with the water in a covered pot for 6 to 8 minutes until they open. Discard any that stay shut.
2. Strain the liquid through a cloth to catch grit, and keep every drop. Chop the clam meat.
3. Render the salt pork in a heavy pot over medium heat until crisp. Lift out the crisp bits and reserve.
4. Cook the onion and celery in the fat for 8 minutes without browning. Stir in the flour and cook 2 minutes.
5. Add the clam liquor gradually, whisking, then the potatoes, bay, and thyme. Simmer 15 minutes until the potatoes are tender and some have started to break down and thicken things.
6. Add the milk and cream and bring to a bare simmer. Never let it boil.
7. Add the chopped clams and butter, heat through for 2 minutes only, and season with white pepper. Serve with the crisp pork, chives, and crackers.

**Cook's note:** Clams toughen in about a minute of hard cooking. They go in last, and the pot comes off the heat right after.

## 14. Maine Lobster Roll

Cold lobster in a light dressing, in a griddled split-top bun. The Connecticut version uses warm butter instead. Both are good, but this is the Maine one.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 15 min**

**Ingredients**
- 4 live lobsters, 500 g each, or 500 g cooked lobster meat
- 4 split-top hot dog buns
- 60 g butter, softened
- 3 tbsp mayonnaise, good quality
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 celery stick, very finely diced
- 1 tbsp chopped chives
- ¼ tsp celery salt
- Black pepper
- Lettuce leaves, optional

**Method**

1. Boil the lobsters in heavily salted water for 8 minutes, then plunge into ice water to stop the cooking.
2. Pick out the tail, claw, and knuckle meat. Knuckle meat is the sweetest and most people throw it out.
3. Cut into large chunks, roughly 2 cm. Chopping it small ruins the whole experience.
4. Chill the meat thoroughly, at least an hour.
5. Toss gently with the mayonnaise, lemon, celery, chives, celery salt, and pepper. Use just enough mayo to coat, not to bind. You should still see lobster, not dressing.
6. Butter the flat outer sides of the buns and griddle in a pan until golden and crisp on both sides.
7. Line with lettuce if you like, then fill generously so the meat sits above the rim. Eat immediately.

**Cook's note:** Dress it right before serving. Lobster sitting in mayonnaise for an hour goes watery and loses its texture.

## 15. Poutine

Chips, cheese curds, gravy. Quebec's contribution to the world, and the details that matter are fresh squeaky curds and a gravy hot enough to soften them without melting them into soup.

**Serves 4 | Prep 20 min | Cook 40 min**

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg floury potatoes, cut into 1 cm chips
- Neutral oil for frying
- 400 g fresh cheese curds, at room temperature

*Gravy*
- 40 g butter
- 40 g plain flour
- 500 ml beef stock
- 250 ml chicken stock
- 1 tbsp ketchup
- 1 tbsp cider vinegar
- 2 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tsp black pepper
- Salt

**Method**

1. Soak the cut chips in cold water for 30 minutes, then dry them completely.
2. Make a roux: melt the butter, whisk in the flour, and cook over medium heat for 5 minutes until it turns the colour of peanut butter.
3. Add both stocks gradually, whisking out lumps as you go. Add ketchup, vinegar, Worcestershire, and pepper.
4. Simmer 15 minutes until it coats a spoon. Season and keep it hot.
5. Fry the chips at 150°C for 5 minutes until soft and pale. Drain and cool at least 20 minutes.
6. Raise the oil to 190°C and fry again for 3 minutes until deep golden and crunchy. Salt immediately.
7. Pile the chips in a bowl, scatter the curds over, and pour the very hot gravy on top. Serve at once.

**Cook's note:** Cold curds won't soften properly. Take them out of the fridge an hour ahead, and don't substitute grated mozzarella, since the squeak is half the reason poutine exists.

## 16. Texas Smoked Brisket

Salt, pepper, oak smoke, twelve hours. Central Texas barbecue is deliberately minimal, which means there's nothing to hide behind.

**Serves 12 | Prep 30 min | Cook 12 hr plus 2 hr rest**

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole packer brisket, 5 to 6 kg, point and flat attached
- 6 tbsp coarse black pepper, 16 mesh
- 5 tbsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp garlic powder, optional
- Post oak or hickory wood
- Butcher paper, unwaxed

**Method**

1. Trim the fat cap to about 6 mm, remove the hard deckle fat between the muscles, and round off the edges so nothing thin sticks out to burn.
2. Mix the rub and coat every surface heavily. It should look like the meat is wearing a coat. Rest at room temperature 1 hour.
3. Get the smoker steady at 120°C with clean thin blue smoke. Thick white smoke means bad combustion and it will make the meat taste acrid.
4. Put the brisket on fat side up, point end toward the fire. Leave it alone for 6 hours.
5. Around 70°C internal, the temperature will stop climbing. This is the stall, it's evaporative cooling, and it can last 4 hours. Wait it out or wrap through it.
6. Wrap tightly in butcher paper once the bark is dark and firm and doesn't smudge when you touch it. Return to the smoker.
7. Pull at 96 to 99°C, when a probe slides into the flat like it's going into room-temperature butter. Rest wrapped in a cooler for at least 2 hours. Slice the flat pencil-thick against the grain, then turn the point 90 degrees and slice that separately.

**Cook's note:** Temperature is a guide, feel is the answer. Two briskets of the same weight can finish 3°C apart, and probe tenderness is the only reliable test.

## 17. Apple Pie

Double crust, mixed apples, and enough thickener that the filling holds when you cut it. Cold butter, cold water, minimum handling.

**Serves 8 | Prep 40 min plus 2 hr chilling | Cook 1 hr**

**Ingredients**

*Pastry*
- 375 g plain flour
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- 250 g very cold unsalted butter, cubed
- 120 ml ice water, plus 1 tbsp cider vinegar

*Filling*
- 1.4 kg apples, half tart (Granny Smith) and half sweet (Braeburn or Honeycrisp), peeled and sliced 8 mm
- 150 g sugar, half of it brown
- 3 tbsp cornstarch
- 1½ tsp cinnamon, ¼ tsp nutmeg, ¼ tsp allspice
- Juice and zest of ½ lemon
- ¼ tsp salt
- 20 g butter, cubed
- Egg wash and demerara sugar

**Method**

1. Cut the butter into the flour, sugar, and salt until you have pieces ranging from pea-sized to almond-sized. Those flat sheets of butter become the flaky layers.
2. Add the ice water and vinegar and toss with a fork until it just holds when squeezed. Divide, flatten into discs, wrap, and chill 2 hours.
3. Toss the apples with the sugar, spices, lemon, and salt. Leave 45 minutes, then drain the juice into a pan.
4. Reduce that juice to about 4 tbsp of syrup, then toss it back through the apples with the cornstarch. This step is why the pie won't be watery.
5. Roll the bottom crust, line a 23 cm pie dish, and chill. Pile in the filling, mounding it high, and dot with butter.
6. Roll the top crust, lay it over, trim, seal, crimp, and cut steam vents. Chill 30 minutes. A cold pie going into a hot oven holds its shape.
7. Bake at 220°C for 20 minutes, then 190°C for 40 to 50 minutes until the juices bubble thickly through the vents. Cool at least 4 hours before cutting.

**Cook's note:** Four hours of cooling feels absurd and it's the difference between a slice and a puddle. The starch needs to set completely, and a warm pie has not set.
